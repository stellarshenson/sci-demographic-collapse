# Fertility Policy That Works - an Evidence Brief for Polish Policymakers

**Purpose**: a plain-language summary of a computational research campaign - 419
pre-registered, tested claims across 44 experiment rounds, with the full evidence trail in
[the experiments log](experiments/demographic-collapse-experiments.md). It says which fertility
interventions hold up under testing, which do not, and why. It ranks levers and explains
mechanisms; it does not forecast birth counts. Written for decision-makers, and for researchers
who want the testable claim behind each statement.

## The five findings that matter most

- **Half of everything tested failed.** Of 419 claims put to the model under pre-registered
  pass/fail criteria, nearly half came back partially or fully refuted - and the casualties are
  disproportionately the politically attractive levers: cash bonuses, awareness campaigns,
  bans, happiness programs. The refutations are the most valuable content of this brief: every
  lever refuted on paper is money that never gets wasted in practice
- **Poland's binding constraint is not the price of a child.** In the model's calibrated
  placement Poland sits inside the cultural trap (a self-reinforcing state where small families
  are the norm and ordinary incentives no longer bite), below the wellbeing floor (the level of
  hardship beneath which family formation starts to be suppressed), and with the most turbulent family norms (the
  fastest year-to-year wobble in family behaviour) of the eight countries studied (E25, E42,
  E43). Money alone does not touch any of those three facts
- **Cash is a mirage; structure is not.** One-off or eroding cash transfers produce a birth-rate
  bump that borrows births from the future and changes almost no completed families. Permanent,
  universal, in-kind structure (childcare, housing supply, security) persists
- **Go decisively or endow permanently.** Cultural interventions either cross a threshold and
  lock in, or they mostly evaporate. The worst value for money is the half-hearted middle - a
  campaign too small to tip and too short to compound
- **Do not wait for a warning.** The drift into the trap rarely announces itself: in the model's
  stress tests, at least two thirds of cultural tipping events arrived with no detectable
  early-warning signal, and a standard 40-year national statistical series would catch the
  approach only about once in a hundred tries (E44). Policy must act on position, not on alarm

## What does not work (tested and failed)

Each entry names the failure mode and the evidence round; details and numbers are in the log.

- **Cash transfers as the main lever** - the tempo mirage: births are pulled forward, completed
  family size barely moves; the effect erodes as the payment is capitalized into prices and
  expectations (E15, E17, E19). The campaign's Poland-calibrated finding is blunt: **cash plus
  childcare, alone, does not lift Poland out of its trajectory** (E15) - essentially the
  architecture Poland has already built, the 500+/800+ transfers together with Maluch+
  childcare, judged here strictly as a fertility instrument; the model says nothing about these
  programs' poverty-reduction effects, which are outside its scope
- **Means-tested and temporary designs** - geometry beats magnitude: universal beats
  means-tested (the eligibility cliff destroys the incentive where it matters), permanent beats
  temporary, national beats local, in-kind beats eroding cash (E17)
- **Awareness campaigns and top-down status propaganda** - backfire because people push back
  against being told to want children; the status of parenthood moves bottom-up or not at all
  (E15, E16, E18)
- **Bans and coercion** - a tutoring-style ban backfires immediately through evasion (E16), and
  coercive natalism reverses within generations: what gains births in generation one is repaid
  with interest by generation four (E30, E34)
- **Raising the cost of exit** (divorce restrictions, lock-in incentives) - removes the
  bargaining safety that lets couples form in the first place; derived independently twice
  (E17, E38)
- **"Make people happier" programs** - the mood boost fades within about two years (people
  adapt) and never reaches decade-scale family decisions, and no version of the
  happiness-as-fertility-lever story survived testing (E42)
- **Education-attainment tinkering** - the diploma is a marker, not a mechanism; income, not
  degrees, carries education's fertility effect (E22, E42)

## What works, in cost order (for Poland's position)

Poland's placement - inside the cultural trap, below the wellbeing floor, high norm turbulence -
selects the levers. Ordered roughly by fertility gain per unit of combined fiscal, coercion and side-effect cost:

1. **Legal recognition of cohabiting couples** - the campaign's best bang-per-buck lever
   overall: near-zero fiscal cost, removes friction at the exact bottleneck (partnership
   formation), and part of the three-lever core below (E20)
2. **The three-lever core: inequality compression + gender equity at home + legal recognition** -
   removing levers one at a time shows this bundle keeps essentially the full gain of much
   larger packages at about a quarter of the cost (E20). Concretely: inequality compression
   means blunting the winner-take-all education and labor-market race that enforces the
   career-first script; gender equity at home means instruments like use-it-or-lose-it paternity
   leave - which the campaign found moves the home-equity norm this lever runs on, not births
   directly. The equity lever is load-bearing: it is what flips the education-fertility gradient
   from penalty to premium (E22, E42)
3. **Male earnings prospects at the bottom of the distribution** - the strongest single causal
   lever in the literature the model consumes (regional labor-demand shocks raise births,
   largely independently of marriage); income, not credentials (E22, E36)
4. **Housing supply, not purchase subsidies** - subsidies capitalize into prices; supply
   relieves both affordability and the household-formation feedback. One warning attached: in
   trapped countries the ongoing decline of couple formation eats the housing dividend from
   inside - supply must ride with the partnership-formation levers above, not replace them (E16, E38, E39)
5. **Economic security as floor protection** - Poland's modelled parents sit below the wellbeing
   floor, and in the model security is the one wellbeing input people never adapt to (a mood
   boost fades; a security shift persists) - so employment and housing security do lasting
   fertility work that comfort spending does not (E42, E43)
6. **De-sequencing education** - student-parent housing, campus childcare, part-time degree
   paths: removing the forced "finish first, then family" sequence beat an equal-relief cut in
   education itself in all three countries tested, Poland among them (E42)

Two cross-cutting rules: **earlier is roughly twice as effective** (the demographic momentum
clock runs regardless, E20), and **complementary levers stack while same-channel levers
saturate** - one lever per bottleneck, not three on the same one (E18).

## The recession warning

The model's newest stress finding (E44): a deterioration in economic security *combined with*
social amplification of parental hardship tips far more of the population into the childfree
norm than either alone - and background turbulence amplifies this pair strongly. Translation:
**during downturns, protecting parents' economic floor is fertility policy**, and it is exactly
when it is most tempting to cut.

## What to monitor

- **Not the national average.** Early-warning indicators on one aggregate series are effectively
  blind - they catch a genuine approach only about once in a hundred tries at realistic data
  length (E44)
- **Subgroup dispersion** - our model finds the spread across groups announces a cultural tip
  slightly before the average moves (E44); monitor family-formation indicators by region,
  cohort and education band
- **Revealed behaviour over declared attitudes** - marriage and cohabitation rates, first-birth
  timing, childcare demand; declared family ideals systematically overstate the realized state
  (E38)

## For researchers (the testable core)

Each headline above rests on parameters a Polish research program could measure directly:
the male-earnings marriage elasticity in Polish regional data; the de-sequencing effect in
enrollment cohorts; the equity-at-home gradient interaction; the wellbeing-floor threshold in
panel wellbeing data; housing-tightness effects on partnership formation; community retention
rates behind the long-run cultural floor. The model tells you in advance which quantities move
the answer - that is what it is for.

## Honest limits (read before acting)

- This is a **ranking and mechanism instrument, not a forecasting oracle**: it licenses "lever A
  dominates lever B in Poland's position, for this mechanism" - never "policy X delivers Y
  births by year Z"
- The model **failed its own recovery backtest honestly**: it reproduces collapses and timing
  shifts faithfully but has no mechanism for recovery episodes, so it ranks scenarios and
  levers rather than forecasting upturns
- It is **national-aggregate and descriptive**: sub-national divergence is unresolved, and
  parameters need validation against Polish registry data before a ministry acts on magnitudes
- **Poland's trap placement is a structural prior**, and the model's own audit records that one
  attitude indicator points the other way - the lever ranking above is conditional on that
  placement, which is precisely the kind of question Polish survey data could settle
- Every claim here carries a pre-registered test in
  [the experiments log](experiments/demographic-collapse-experiments.md); the model design and
  its full limitations are in [the SOTA document](demographic-collapse-sota.md)

## The one-paragraph takeaway

The audits behind these limits overturned no verdict; the one open conditionality - Poland's
trap placement - is named above and can be settled with Polish data. If Poland acts on one
thing from this brief, it is
the three-lever core - legal recognition of cohabiting couples, gender equity at home, and
blunting the winner-take-all race - started now rather than after a warning that, on the evidence
above, will probably never come. And in the next downturn, defend parents' economic floor first: on this
model, that is fertility policy at the moment it matters most.
