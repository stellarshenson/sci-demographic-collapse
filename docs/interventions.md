# Graded Interventions - What Actually Reverses Demographic Collapse, and How

A decision-maker's ranking of the interventions tested in the model (Notebook 10, experiment E15 - fifty
hypotheses), graded against the (quasi-)experimental literature and the calibrated model. Unlike a generic
policy wishlist, every lever here is **specific**, its **mechanism of effect** is spelled out as a causal
chain, and its **side effects** are named and sourced. Full evidence per lever is in
[`experiments/demographic-collapse-experiments.md`](experiments/demographic-collapse-experiments.md) (E15) and
the source digests in [`../references/papers/`](../references/papers/).

## The one lesson first: why the "obvious" levers fail alone - the Poland case

Poland ran the textbook playbook and it failed. It built free public nurseries (Maluch+) and paid a universal
child benefit (500+, later 800+) costing more than 93 billion euros - and its total fertility rate went 1.32
(2015) → 1.47 (a brief blip) → about **1.1 by 2025**, third-lowest in the EU. Korea is the same story at
larger scale: 270 billion dollars of pro-natal spending, and a birth rate that fell every single year to 0.72.

Cash and even childcare do not work *in isolation* because they do not touch the binding constraint: a
**life-script that puts career, credentials and economic security first and family a distant second**,
enforced by a winner-take-all economy. Doepke and Zilibotti name the mechanism - intensive, high-stakes
"tiger" parenting is a *rational* response to high inequality, so a competitive child becomes ruinously
expensive in time and money, and people have fewer. This is why the grading below rewards levers that attack
that root, and marks down levers that only subsidize the symptom.

## How the stars work

Each 0-5 rating combines five things: **effect** (model + measured), **durability** (structural vs fading),
**evidence** (tier 1 quasi-experiments to tier 3 extrapolated), **keystone-aim** (does it touch coupling / the
extensive margin), and **net of side effects**. Five stars = strong, durable, well-evidenced, aimed at the
root, with tolerable side effects. Ratings are the model's judgement, not a costing.

## How any lever moves the number (the mechanism model)

Total fertility is the product of two margins - `TFR = (1 − ρ) · P̄` - where `ρ` is childlessness (the
extensive margin, the share of women who never become mothers) and `P̄` is completed parity among mothers (the
intensive margin). Every intervention is a lift placed on the age-specific fertility schedule through one of
these channels, then run through the calibrated age-structured projection to 2100. So each lever's causal
chain reads: **policy → a behavioural parameter → a margin (ρ or P̄, or timing) → TFR → 2100 population**, and
- because the system is coupled - it also perturbs other variables (the side effects). One structural fact
governs all of them: near the separatrix a small reduction in childlessness *flips* a society from collapse to
recovery, while deep in the basin the identical push does nothing (`nb10_interactions.png`). Leverage is a
function of position.

## Tier 1 - attack the root: the career-first arms race (★★★★★)

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ★★★★★ | **Compress the winner-take-all stakes** - wage compression, credential-blind hiring, a strong safety net | lower inequality → parents stop the intensive-parenting arms race (Doepke) → time+money cost per child falls → completed parity P̄ rises | Doepke-Zilibotti; low-inequality Nordics carry higher fertility | growth/incentive trade-off; politically hard |
| ★★★★★ | **Dismantle the education arms race** - hagwon curfew + private-tutoring cap + broaden university admission beyond the single exam (CSAT) | private-tutoring spend (27 trillion won, "edupoverty" 40%) is what makes a competitive child unaffordable → cap it → perceived child cost falls and youth are freed from the exam treadmill → P̄ up, coupling up | Korea (Suneung, 85,000 hagwons); Korea cutting exam "killer questions" | underground/shadow tutoring - tutoring markets are "locked-in" (Oxford Rev. 2021); the rich still buy advantage |
| ★★★★☆ | **Break the overwork norm** - enforce a real hours cap, a right to disconnect, penalize presenteeism/gwarosa | Korea works 2,069 h/yr; long hours crowd out pairing and childrearing ("a baby is the end of your career") → cut them → time and energy return → coupling + parity | women at 35-45 h/week have higher pregnancy odds; fathers can co-parent | output/productivity concerns (the 69-hour-week backlash); enforcement is hard |

### Details

**Compress the winner-take-all stakes (★★★★★).** The deepest lever, because it is upstream of the others.
Doepke and Zilibotti show that intensive, achievement-oriented parenting is not irrational vanity but a
rational response to high inequality: when the gap between "made it" and "did not" is huge, parents pour
unlimited time and money into each child to secure its future - which makes each child hugely expensive, so
they have fewer. Compressing the returns to the tournament (wage compression, hiring that does not fetishize
elite credentials, a safety net that lowers the downside of "failure") relaxes parenting, lowers the real cost
of a child, and raises completed parity. **Predicted effect**: a durable intensive-margin lift (model
H90). **Side effect**: the classic equality-efficiency trade-off, and it is politically the hardest of all -
but the low-inequality, high-fertility Nordic pattern is the existence proof. Source:
`[paper digest] ... Doepke intensive parenting` (Love, Money and Parenting).

**Dismantle the education arms race (★★★★★).** Specific to East Asia but generalizing wherever credential
competition rules. A single exam, Korea's Suneung, can define a life; 85,000 hagwons and a 21-billion-dollar
private-tutoring market turn raising a "competitive" child into edupoverty for four families in ten. The chain:
cap the arms race (hagwon curfews, tutoring-spend limits, and - crucially - broadening university admission so
one exam no longer decides everything) → the marginal cost of a child collapses and young people are freed from
a decade on the exam treadmill → parity and coupling both rise. **Predicted effect**: model H88/H89, an
intensive-margin lift plus a coupling gain. **Side effect**: tutoring goes underground (the market is
"locked-in" and hard to kill), and wealthier families still buy advantage - so it must be paired with the
inequality lever above. Sources: Korea education-fever reporting; Oxford Review of Education (locked-in
tutoring).

**Break the overwork norm (★★★★☆).** Distinct from generic "work-life balance": the target is the *cultural
expectation of visible overwork*. Korea's 2,069 annual hours (second in the OECD) and its presenteeism leave no
time or energy to form a couple or raise a child - Korean women name work hours as the single biggest factor in
whether they intend to get pregnant, and fathers working long hours cannot co-parent. Enforce a genuine hours
cap, a right to disconnect, and penalties for presenteeism → time and energy return to family life →
coupling and parity rise. **Predicted effect**: model H91/H100, a both-margin lift, strongest for Korea/Japan.
**Side effect**: output and competitiveness concerns (the backlash that greeted the proposed 69-hour week), and
enforcement against an entrenched norm is hard. Source: `[paper] working hours pregnancy intention Korea, 2023`.

## Tier 1 - the enabling structural levers (★★★★★ - ★★★★)

| Stars | Intervention | Mechanism of effect | Evidence | Main side effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ★★★★★ | **Universal near-free childcare** from ~12 months, high coverage | removes the money-and-career cost of a child during the intensive early years → P̄ up; frees mothers' careers → less postponement | Bergsvik 2021 (largest, sustained); model's strongest single lever | fiscal cost; alone it is not enough (see Poland) |
| ★★★★★ | **Gender equality in the home** - equal domestic load, norms + gender-aware leave | the dual-margin master lever (more mothers *and* higher parity) once above the equity threshold | Myrskylä J-curve | J-curve: *below* a gender-equity threshold the transition worsens the dual burden first |
| ★★★★☆ | **Family-size housing** - build/subsidize 3-bedroom-plus units for young families | space and security for children → both margins; housing cost is a first-order delay driver | housing drove ~half the US fertility drop; 3BR+ raise births 2.3× small units | subsidy can capitalize into prices unless supply expands |
| ★★★★☆ | **Early-parenthood security + job guarantee** | caps the motherhood career penalty (removes *risk*, not price) → first births pulled earlier (lower ρ), penalty neutralized (higher P̄) | Kleven child penalty persists 20 yrs (21-61%) | the Nordic ceiling: security alone plateaus at ~1.5-1.7 |

Details for these four mirror the mechanism column: childcare lifts `P̄` by removing the early-years cost
(source `[paper] family policies fertility systematic review Bergsvik, 2021`); home gender equality lifts both
margins but only above the J-curve threshold, and *below* it the first-gender-revolution dual burden depresses
fertility further - which is why it is a genuine lever, not a slogan (`[paper digest] ... Myrskyla`); family-
size housing lowers the space/security barrier to a *second and third* child (`[paper] housing affordability
marriage childbearing Wachter, 2024`); and early-parenthood security removes the downside risk documented by
the child-penalty literature so people can have children *before* the career is "secured"
(`[paper] child penalty atlas Kleven Landais, 2023`).

## Tier 2 - resequence the script and mend coupling (★★★★ - ★★★)

| ★★★☆☆ | **Regulate the attention economy** - minor social-media limits, dating-app anti-addiction design, right to disconnect - *and rebuild offline venues* | screen time displaced socializing, gamified apps bred commitment-phobia, and apps disintermediated matchmakers without raising the pairing rate → reverse all three → coupling | Twenge; Rosenfeld | app regulation *alone* is necessary but not sufficient - offline meeting must be rebuilt |

### Details (Tier 2)

**Career-neutral parenthood + break the mutual-postponement trap (★★★★☆).** This is the specific fix for the
dynamic where both young men and women now lock in career and long hours *before* any life choice - each
waiting, each rewarded for waiting, until the fertile window closes (a financial-security worry raises the odds
of delaying a first birth by 128%). The lever makes having a child mid-training or mid-career *not* a
competitive loss for either partner: promotion-neutral leave, on-ramps, stopped clocks that protect *promotion*
and not just tenure. **Predicted effect**: model H92/H94, an extensive-margin (earlier-birth) lift. **Side
effect - and it is decisive**: a naive *gender-neutral* clock-stop backfires. Stearns et al. found gender-
neutral tenure-clock stopping raised men's tenure rates and lowered women's, widening the gap, because men used
the extra time to publish while women did the caregiving. So the policy must be gender-*aware*, protecting
mothers specifically - otherwise it deepens the very penalty it means to remove. Source:
`[paper digest] ... tenure clock` (Stearns).

**De-escalate the gender war (★★★★☆).** The strongest coupling lever for Korea specifically, and the
constructive mirror of the REFUTED "roll back gender equality" lever. Perceived gender conflict now directly
shapes young Koreans' marriage and childbearing attitudes (Lee 2025), dramatized by the 4B movement (young
women rejecting marriage, childbirth, dating and sex with men) and a young-adult gender divide in which men
name "feminism" as the thing women must "fix." Reducing that polarization - through genuine equality, not
backlash - restores viable matches. **Predicted effect**: model H85, the largest coupling lift for Korea.
**Side effect**: the effort can be captured by either faction; it only works if it lowers conflict rather than
picking a side. Source: `[paper digest] ... Korea gender conflict Lee, 2025`.

**Regulate the attention economy (★★★☆☆).** The modern wound, through three distinct channels: screen time
displaced the in-person socializing where relationships form (Twenge), the gamified infinite swipe bred choice-
paralysis and commitment-phobia, and apps displaced the friends and community who used to make matches without
raising the pairing rate (Rosenfeld). Minor social-media limits, dating-app anti-addiction design (the loot-box
regulation parallel) and a right to disconnect all help - **but** the honest chain ends only if the society
also rebuilds the offline places where the young meet; app regulation alone leaves the market as thin as it
found it. **Predicted effect**: model H64-H66, a tier-3 coupling lift. Sources:
`[paper] dating apps mating Rosenfeld, 2019`, `[paper digest] ... Twenge`.

**Recognize cohabitation + remove the marriage tax penalty (★★★☆☆).** France's PACS civil union shows that
recognizing cohabitation rather than privileging marriage keeps the union-fertility link intact - France
carries over 40% non-marital births and Europe's healthier fertility. Removing the marriage tax penalty lowers
the financial cost of pairing. **Predicted effect**: model H78/H82, a coupling lift. **Side effect**:
recognition without accompanying support can simply formalize fragile unions. Source:
`[paper digest] ... France PACS`.

**Sustained replacement migration (★★★★☆).** The fastest lever, because a migrant arrives already grown and of
childbearing age and reaches straight into the hollow middle of the pyramid - no generational lag. It is the
single largest population lever in the model and what keeps the US near flat. **Side effect / why four not five
stars**: it refills numbers without repairing the birth deficit or the coupling keystone - a bridge, not a cure
- and it carries real integration and social-cohesion friction and depends on sustained political will.

## Side effects and interactions - the full picture

No lever acts alone. Two general facts, both grounded and both in the model:

- **The system is coupled** - a push on one variable propagates through the others. In the Seldon manifold,
  lowering childlessness `ρ` also lifts coupling and births, which feed back to sustain the gain - but only
  where the population base is large enough. So the *same* keystone push flips the fate near the ridge and does
  nothing deep in the basin (`nb10_interactions.png`). This is the mechanistic reason the deepest cases
  (Korea, Japan) can be bent but not reversed within the century.
- **Several levers carry documented countervailing effects**: cash cuts child poverty but reduces female
  labour supply (Poland 500+); restricting divorce raises women's suicide and domestic violence (Stevenson-
  Wolfers); a gender-neutral career-clock stop widens the gender gap (Stearns); capping hagwons breeds shadow
  tutoring; restricting contraception tripled maternal mortality (Romania); and gender equality, below its
  J-curve threshold, worsens the dual burden before it helps. A serious program pairs each lever with the one
  that offsets its side effect.

## Defection-robustness and the side-effect frontier (E16)

Gross effect sizes lie, because interventions are evaded. E16 adds a **defection parameter δ** to every lever:
the net a society gets is the gross times the compliant share, plus a backfire term that can invert the sign
when the rich defect. This reorders the whole menu, and it answers the question the arms-race levers really
turn on - not "how strong" but "can it be defected from".

- **A ban backfires; an un-buyable lever does not** - Korea outlawed private tutoring in 1980; tutoring went
  underground, only the wealthy could pay the covert premium, and inequality - the fuel of the race - widened.
  Net −0.235: the sign flips. The levers that work make the prize un-buyable. A **threshold-and-lottery-band**
  admits a random draw from everyone above a competence bar, so the payoff is flat above the bar and the last
  dollar of tutoring buys nothing (the reward-gradient above the bar collapses to zero - a 100% arms-race
  deflation). A **multi-dimensional** admission rewards aptitude, grit and critical thinking - traits with low
  purchase-elasticity - so family money stops converting into rank. Both truncate the race at its source; both
  carry δ≈0 because a lottery seat cannot be bought.
- **The defection screen** - re-ranking every lever by net-after-defection reshuffles the order. Bans,
  propaganda, wealth caps and marriage-first exhortation fall net-negative. Inequality compression, lottery
  bucketing, universal motherhood-penalty removal, housing supply and structural defaults rise to the top. The
  Norwegian father's quota is the proof of the principle: a non-transferable, use-it-or-lose-it default took
  uptake from under 3% to 97% where every voluntary equivalent failed. What a state can enforce, not what
  sounds strong, is what bends the curve. And when defection is inevitable, taxing the defection channel and
  recycling it turns a ban's leakage into a structural transfer.
- **The Western natural experiments** - Israel is the only rich country above replacement; even its secular
  women sit near 2.0 without coercion, because a child is the near-universal default and universal IVF insures
  the biological runway. Hungary spends 5-6% of GDP and its rise was ~85% tempo - the completed family barely
  moved and the rate fell back. Housing is opposite-signed by tenure, so the lever is supply for young renters,
  not a price subsidy to owners. Student debt delays family directly.
- **The frontier** - stacked against their side effects, the coercive levers (a childlessness tax, a wealth
  cap, propaganda, a tutoring ban) are dominated: they either go net-negative or impose welfare costs no
  fertility gain justifies. The efficient frontier is structural and un-buyable - inequality compression,
  lottery bucketing, universal penalty removal, housing supply, the IVF default. On the Seldon manifold the
  defection-robust bundle crosses the separatrix near the ridge where the fragile bundle stalls.

## Geometry over magnitude - how a lever is delivered decides its fate (E17)

E17 swept each lever along its design axis instead of testing one point, and read where the optimum sits. The
recurring finding is that *delivery*, not spend, governs the outcome - and that the optima are diverse, so
there is no lever a designer simply turns up to maximum.

- **The five delivery axes** - the same transfer succeeds or fails on how it is shaped. **Universal beats
  means-tested**: a means-test is a cliff, a perverse "stay-below-threshold" incentive, and non-take-up eats the
  targeting saving. **In-kind beats cash**: services persist where cash erodes, so form matters more than size.
  **Credible permanence** earns the full response: fertility is a 25-year bet, so a policy believed temporary is
  discounted to tempo (this is what "durability" really means). **National beats local**: a municipal baby
  bonus relocates births rather than raising them - beggar-thy-neighbor. **State-funded beats an employer
  mandate**: making employers pay for maternity triggers statistical hiring discrimination against young women,
  widening the very motherhood penalty it meant to relieve, so the net turns negative
- **The outside option is the valve** - the sharpest mechanism in the batch. Rewarding union *duration* works
  only as de-risking (economic security, childcare, school finance-and-communication education that cuts the
  stressors which dissolve unions - a PREP RCT cut divorce to a third), never as lock-in. Raising the cost of
  leaving - covenant marriage, an adultery penalty, a divorce restriction - backfires: the credible right to
  exit is what hands the weaker partner bargaining power and de-escalates coercion without ever being used
  (no-fault divorce cut female suicide up to ~20% and domestic violence ~30%). Remove the exit and you remove
  the incentive it was meant to strengthen. Anniversary bonuses and longevity medals are symbolic-null; a porn
  ban is a null fertility lever and high-evasion (the sex-recession is partnership decline, not pornography)
- **Name the money in coupling** - a quarter of the coupling fertility premium is literally affordability: two
  live on ~1.5x one income, and single-parent precarity (poverty ~5.8x couples) suppresses higher-order births.
  De-risking solo parenthood helps but a benefit-cliff past ~50% support perversely delays cohabitation, so the
  optimum is interior - support that complements the couple, not one that substitutes for it
- **The controversial extreme is dominated** - a paid surrogate-carrier *class* attacks the career penalty in
  principle, but the demand gain is a rounding error at population scale, supply-side displacement bounds the
  net, and the psychological/commodification and stratified-reproduction cost is the steepest in the campaign;
  India ran the commercial-hub version and banned it. The exportable part of Israel's success is subsidised IVF
  for a couple's own gestation, not a carrier class

## The rule that governs all of them

No single lever is a cure, and the "obvious" ones - cash and childcare - demonstrably fail alone, as Poland and
Korea prove. What separates the levers that carry a recovery from those that fade is not their size but their
**durability**, their aim at the **root** (the career-first arms race) and the **coupling keystone**, and above
all **timing**. The same maximal, well-aimed bundle returns societies still near the ridge (the US, France,
Italy) to growth but only bends the deepest cases (Korea, Japan) from collapse to a shallow decline within this
century - because leverage is a function of position, and the window closes one birth cohort at a time.
