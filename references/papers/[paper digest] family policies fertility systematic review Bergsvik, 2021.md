## Summary

A 57-study systematic review of quasi-experimental and experimental evidence on how family policy affects fertility across Europe, the US, Canada and Australia since 1970.

## Key findings

- Childcare expansions increase completed fertility - the largest and most sustained effect
- Longer parental leave increases fertility in both the short and long run
- Cash transfers have only temporary effects; about half of studies find no effect
- Paternity quotas show no significant fertility effect in the main Nordic samples

## Relevance to this project

The backbone of the E15 strength catalogue and its ordering (childcare > leave > cash > bonus). Grounds H51 (cash weakest), H57 (ordering), H54 (durable beats temporary) and the H68 paternity-quota null.
