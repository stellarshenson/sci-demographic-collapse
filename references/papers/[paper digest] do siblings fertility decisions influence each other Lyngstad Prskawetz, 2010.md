# [Paper digest] Do siblings' fertility decisions influence each other?

## Summary

Asks whether a sibling having a child triggers the respondent's own childbearing - social contagion within the family, distinct from the shared-upbringing transmission that Kolk (2015) shows is confounded. Uses Norwegian population-wide administrative registers, more than 110,000 sibling pairs, with fertility, education, income and marital histories. Continuous-time hazard models for first and second births, with a sibling's birth entered as a time-varying covariate and a shared-frailty term for the sibling pair to net out common family background.

## Key findings

- **A sibling's birth raises the respondent's own hazard of a first birth, concentrated in the ~3 years after the sibling's birth, then fading** - a short-lived contagion pulse on the *timing* of entry into parenthood
- The effect is **net of parental fertility and shared family frailty**, so it is a genuine cross-sibling social-interaction effect, not just inherited preferences
- **Stronger the older the sibling is relative to the respondent** (younger siblings are influenced by older ones more than the reverse)
- **For second births, the sibling-contagion effect is essentially null** - contagion operates on the transition to parenthood, not on progression
- Direction and timing echo the friends-contagion finding of Balbo-Barban (2014, already in the library): a close peer's birth raises own transition risk, peaking within 1-3 years

## Relevance to this project

This is the one genuinely causal *intragenerational* kin-fertility effect that survives confound controls - but it is a tempo/timing effect on first births that fades within three years, not a lift to completed family size. It belongs with the existing norm/contagion channel (E43 `N`, Balbo-Barban) as a short-run synchroniser, not as a structural parity multiplier. It does not, on its own, justify a self-reinforcing family-size channel.
