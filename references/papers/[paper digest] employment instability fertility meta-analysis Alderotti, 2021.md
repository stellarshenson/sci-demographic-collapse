# [Paper digest] Employment Instability and Fertility in Europe: A Meta-Analysis

**Original (link)**: [https://read.dukeupress.edu/demography/article/58/3/871/173418](https://read.dukeupress.edu/demography/article/58/3/871/173418) (DOI 10.1215/00703370-9164737)<br>

**Used in**: E18-H163 (employment precarity suppresses fertility)

## Summary

Meta-analysis synthesising European micro-level studies on how employment instability (unemployment, time-limited/fixed-term contracts, economic uncertainty) affects fertility, resolving a fragmented and contradictory literature into a pooled direction and size.

## Key findings

- Employment instability has a nonnegligible negative effect on fertility overall
- Men's unemployment is the most detrimental source; men's time-limited employment matters less than men's unemployment
- For women, holding a fixed-term contract is the configuration least likely to lead to a birth
- The negative effect has strengthened over time and is most severe in Southern Europe, where family/unemployment social protection is weakest
- Failing to control for income and partner characteristics overestimates the effect - income and partnership are proposed mediating mechanisms

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H163: labour-market precarity is a genuine, gendered anti-natal undercurrent - male unemployment and female fixed-term contracts each suppress births, and the damage is worse where the welfare buffer is thin. Points the lever at job security and income stability, not only at direct birth subsidies.
