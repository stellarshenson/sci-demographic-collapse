## Summary

Uses the German Socio-Economic Panel (GSOEP), which repeatedly measures life satisfaction, childbearing events, and Big Five personality, to test whether subjective well-being (SWB) causally predicts having a(nother) child rather than merely correlating through stable personality. Controlling for personality traits, SWB positively predicts subsequent childbearing for both women and men, and the effect is significant and sizeable specifically for the second child - establishing well-being as a genuine antecedent of fertility, not just a consequence.

## Parameters for the model

- **Direction**: SWB (life satisfaction) positively predicts the probability/hazard of a subsequent birth for both genders - the W -> fertility arrow the model needs
- **Parity structure**: the effect is significant and sizeable for the **second child** for both men and women; **first-child** effect is weaker or non-significant once personality is controlled; higher parities not the main channel
- **Non-linearity**: the average marginal effect of SWB on childbearing is strongest at **medium-high** levels of life satisfaction (not at the very top or bottom) - a threshold/saturation shape rather than linear
- **Personality is not the confounder**: Big Five traits explain a large share of SWB variance but do NOT drive the SWB-fertility link - the SWB effect survives controlling for personality, supporting a causal reading
- **Data**: GSOEP panel (Germany), repeated SWB (0-10 life-satisfaction item), childbearing histories, Big Five module
- **Companion result** (Le Moglie, Mencarini, Rapallini 2019, J. Population Economics 32(3):915-952): in Germany the parenthood SWB gain is moderated by income and education - use as the policy/resource interaction on the same channel

## Caveats

Digest-only - exact marginal-effect magnitudes not extractable without the paywalled tables (the abstract reports direction, parity and the medium-high peak but not point estimates). Single-country (Germany), so the second-child specificity may reflect the German institutional context. For the model: encode a positive, saturating W -> birth-hazard coupling that acts mainly on the transition to the second child, with a medium-high-SWB peak, and treat it as (plausibly) causal rather than a personality artifact.
