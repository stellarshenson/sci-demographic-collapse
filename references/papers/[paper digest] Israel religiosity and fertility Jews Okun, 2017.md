## Key mechanism

Okun decomposes the religiosity-fertility gradient among Israeli Jews and asks WHY even secular Jews reproduce near replacement. Her answer is that religion in Israel operates less as personal piety than as cultural, political and institutional power - it shapes family-building norms (early marriage, larger ideal family size), community structure, and state institutions (no civil marriage; religious control of personal status) that reach even the secular. The mechanism is a pronatal FAMILISM default that is decoupled from individual belief and blended with nationalism, so it does not fade with secularisation the way it does elsewhere.

## Main findings

- Cohort fertility by religiosity (women born ~1950s‒60s): Ultra-Orthodox 6.2‒8.0, Religious/Dati ~4.0, Traditional/Masorti 2.5‒3.5, Secular replacement-level or just above
- Among secular respondents aged 25‒49, more than one-fifth had three or more children - anomalous for a developed secular population
- Women's employment is significantly (negatively) related to fertility, BUT controlling for paid work does NOT explain the religiosity differentials → the gap is cultural/institutional, not economic
- Secular identity in Israel is porous: 49% of native-born secular women aged 20‒44 report following kosher laws, and many report occasional observance - "even self-defined secular Jews are not completely secularized"
- Three identified channels: (1) religious COMMUNITY (residential segregation, movement affiliation, organised learning), (2) religion as INSTITUTION (opposition to civil marriage), (3) family-building NORMS (early-marriage ideal, larger ideal family size)

## Method and identification

- Data: 2009 Israel Social Survey; 1,309 Jewish women and 1,238 men aged 25‒49
- Multivariate logistic regression on the odds of having three or more children, controlling for education and employment
- Identifying argument: because the religiosity gradient SURVIVES controls for women's paid work and education, the mechanism is normative/institutional rather than an income/opportunity-cost story - the effect that transfers is the norm, not a wage or childcare price

## Key takeaways

- Pins the pronatal familism norm as the load-bearing, cross-sector channel - it reaches the secular through community, institutions and nationalism, not doctrine
- The employment-does-not-explain-it result is the cleanest evidence the developed-world work-fertility tradeoff is culturally overridden in Israel
- Classification: STRUCTURAL-LEVER in principle (norm N), but INHERENT-CULTURAL in practice - the norm is a historically specific blend of religion + nationalism that policy cannot legislate directly
