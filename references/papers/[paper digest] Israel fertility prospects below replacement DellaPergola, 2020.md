## Key mechanism

Asks whether Israel's above-replacement fertility is a transient artefact or a durable feature, and locates the durable part in a blend of nationalism, familism and existential security operating across ALL sub-populations, not just the religious. DellaPergola's core move is heterogeneity: Israel is not one fertility regime but several coexisting sub-populations, and the manifest co-variation across geographic and ideological divisions points to a shared undercurrent - a collective-future / national-project orientation - layered on top of religiosity. The transferable signal is the norm-plus-security bundle, not doctrine.

## Main findings

- Israel's Jewish TFR re-emerged ABOVE replacement in 2008 after several years dipping below - the only developed setting to reverse upward, evidence the norm is resilient, not decaying
- Fertility runs highest where nationalism is most intense: West Bank settler population TFR above 5 in 2010, explained partly by territorial/ideological nationalism
- Religiosity matters more for fertility when it is closely interconnected with nationalism and familism - among non-Haredi Jews religion acts subtly through that blend, not through observance alone
- Direct security effect is a MINORITY channel: asked whether negative security conditions would change family-size plans, only ~7% of women and ~9% of men said security problems would make them wish for MORE children (a larger share said fewer or no change) - the existential driver is real but not the bulk of the effect
- Heterogeneity is the methodological lesson: separate projection paths are needed per sub-population; a single national TFR misrepresents a bimodal (Haredi vs secular) structure

## Method and identification

- Synthesis of CBS vital statistics, survey data on security-and-family-size attitudes, and sub-population projection scenarios
- Identification is associational/comparative (geography × ideology × religiosity co-variation), not a natural experiment; the security-survey item is self-reported intention, so the direct-security effect is an upper-bound-style attitude measure, not revealed behaviour

## Key takeaways

- Grounds the collective-future / nationalism mechanism and quantifies its DIRECT security component as small (~7‒9% wanting more) - most of the effect is the standing pronatal norm, not acute threat
- Classification: INHERENT-CULTURAL (the nationalism-familism-security blend is historically specific and not a policy dial), with COMPOSITION riding alongside (settlers, Haredi as distinct high-fertility sub-populations)
- Reinforces the modelling discipline: represent Israel as heterogeneous sub-populations, not a single forced TFR
