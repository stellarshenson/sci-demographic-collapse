## Summary

Survey study linking weekly working hours to pregnancy intentions in the world's lowest-fertility country.

## Key findings

- Weekly working hours were the most significant factor affecting pregnancy intentions
- Long hours crowd out marriage, family life and caregiving
- Korea works ~1,915 hours/year, ~200 above the OECD average

## Relevance to this project

The evidence base for H71: cutting extreme working hours (Japan's four-day-week experiment) is a strong, durable, both-margin lever, especially for Korea and Japan.
