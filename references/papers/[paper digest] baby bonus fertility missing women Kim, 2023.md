## Summary

Quasi-experimental evaluation of South Korean municipal cash baby-bonus programs, exploiting variation in transfer size.

## Key findings

- A 10% rise in cash raised birth rates by ~0.6/0.4/0.4% for 1st/2nd/3rd children
- The first-birth response concentrates among younger mothers (25-29)
- Older mothers respond on higher parities (30-34 second, 35-39 third) - an age x parity gradient
- Cash effects are modest overall

## Relevance to this project

The core anchor for H63: cash is not uniformly weak but age-structured, peaking at 25-29, so age-targeted cash buys more per coin than a uniform allowance.
