## Key mechanism

Granovetter defines the strength of a dyadic tie as a combination of time spent, emotional intensity, intimacy and reciprocal services, and shows that the WEAK ties (acquaintances) are the load-bearing conduits for information that reaches far. The argument runs through the "forbidden triad": if A is strongly tied to both B and C, a B‒C tie is almost never absent, so strong ties fold into densely-knit, redundant cliques. A tie that bridges two otherwise-disconnected clusters therefore cannot be strong - all bridges are weak. Novel, non-redundant information (a job opening, a new practice, an idea) flows across these weak bridges; strong ties only recycle what the clique already knows. The macro consequence is that people rich in strong ties but poor in weak ties are sealed inside the provincial news of their intimates.

## Main findings

- Tie strength = time + emotional intensity + intimacy + reciprocal services; weak ties are acquaintanceships, not intimate friendships
- The "forbidden triad" (A‒B and A‒C strong, B‒C absent) is empirically rare, so strong ties cluster into redundant closed triads and cannot serve as bridges
- No strong tie is a (local) bridge - every bridge across clusters is necessarily a weak tie, so weak ties carry all inter-cluster information
- Job-search evidence: among professional/technical/managerial workers who found a job through a personal contact, only 16.7% saw that contact "often", 55.6% "occasionally" and 27.8% "rarely" - roughly five in six jobs travelled a weak tie
- Weak ties raise the reach of any diffusion: an idea seeded through weak ties reaches more people, at greater social distance, than one confined to strong ties
- Communities fragmented into cliques with few weak ties are poor at diffusion and collective mobilisation

## Method and identification

- Theoretical synthesis anchored in a small empirical job-mobility study (professional/technical/managerial workers, N in the low hundreds), plus reinterpretation of prior network studies
- Identifying logic is structural, not causal-experimental: the forbidden-triad regularity forces bridges to be weak, so any observed long-range flow is attributed to weak-tie structure
- Diffusion claims are argued from the topology (bridges vs clique redundancy), later validated by others

## Key takeaways for the model

- Motivates a tie-strength dimension distinct from tie count (H369): a network dominated by few strong ties is a redundancy trap for norm/behaviour spread, while weak ties set the REACH of any fertility-norm diffusion
- Weak ties spread INFORMATION cheaply and far - but this is the SIMPLE-contagion channel; pair with Centola & Macy (2007) for why a costly, reinforcement-hungry behaviour like childbearing does NOT ride weak ties the same way
- Structural parameter: bridge topology, not average degree, governs whether a seeded norm escapes its origin cluster
