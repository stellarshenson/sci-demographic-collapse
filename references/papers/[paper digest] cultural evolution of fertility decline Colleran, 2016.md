## What it gives us

A review, not a new model - but it is the map of the deterministic transmission literature and fixes the vocabulary we should port.

- **Three transmission channels**: vertical (parent→child), oblique (older non-parents, e.g. teachers), horizontal (peers). Our composite bearer is the vertical channel; the "edge-leak"/novelty influx is oblique/horizontal
- Catalogues the formal models: **Ihara & Feldman** (niche construction - a vertically inherited high-education trait raises the oblique-transmission rate of a low-fertility trait); **Kendal et al.** (horizontal transmission interacting with conformity to a high-fertility norm); **Borenstein et al.** (transmission across subpopulation networks)
- Emphasises that **the intergenerational correlation between parents' and children's fertility preferences and outcomes** is the central quantity in maintenance models, and that **transmission fidelity** (high-fidelity vertical vs relaxed) governs whether a norm is stable or erodes - directly our per-component `φ_c`
- Frames fertility decline as an information-flow problem: as populations develop, the balance tips from vertical toward oblique/horizontal, and conformist dynamics can lock in low-fertility norms

## How differential fertility enters

Reviewed rather than derived: in the cited models, high-fertility types are over-represented among the next generation's transmitters, while oblique/horizontal channels (and conformity) can override that vertical signal - the tension our bearer must represent.

## Portability to our ensemble

Confers the naming and structure: split the bearer into vertical fidelity `φ_c` per component and treat secularisation as an oblique/horizontal leak, with conformist bias as an optional nonlinearity. Points to Ihara-Feldman's gating idea (one trait sets another's transmission rate), which motivates our retention/boundary gate.
