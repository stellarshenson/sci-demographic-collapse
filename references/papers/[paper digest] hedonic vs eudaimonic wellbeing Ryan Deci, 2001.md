## Key mechanism

Ryan and Deci partition well-being research into two philosophically distinct traditions. HEDONIC well-being equates the good life with pleasure and satisfaction - well-being is the presence of positive affect, the absence of negative affect, and high life satisfaction. EUDAIMONIC well-being (from Aristotle's *eudaimonia*) equates the good life with meaning, self-realisation and functioning fully as a person - living in accord with one's daimon or true self, not merely feeling good. The review's load-bearing claim for E42 is that the two are empirically separable, not synonyms: a life can score high on pleasure and low on meaning, or the reverse. This is the theoretical warrant for asking whether PURPOSE (eudaimonic) loads on fertility while hedonic satisfaction does not.

## Operational definitions (the two measures)

- **Hedonic = Subjective Well-Being (SWB)**, the Diener/Kahneman tradition: three components - life satisfaction (cognitive) + presence of positive mood + absence of negative mood; "happiness" = the affect balance
- **Eudaimonic = Psychological Well-Being (PWB)**, the Ryff tradition: six dimensions - autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations with others, PURPOSE IN LIFE, and self-acceptance
- **Eudaimonic = Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**, Ryan and Deci's own frame: well-being flows from satisfying three basic psychological needs - autonomy, competence, relatedness; hedonic feeling is a byproduct, not the target
- Waterman's "personal expressiveness" (feeling intensely alive and authentic when doing what one is meant to do) is offered as a further eudaimonic operationalisation distinct from mere pleasure

## Parameters for the model

- Two-construct split is the primary usable structure, not a number: model a MEANING/PURPOSE channel (eudaimonic W) SEPARATE from a satisfaction/affect channel (hedonic W); E42 hypothesis is that only the former loads positively on fertility
- Quantitative divergence (from the cited empirical base, chiefly Ryff & Keyes 1995 and Compton et al. 1996): SWB and PWB are POSITIVELY CORRELATED but factor-analytically DISTINCT - typical between-construct correlations ~0.4‒0.8, and confirmatory factor analysis rejects a single-factor solution
- Age dissociation (the cleanest separation evidence): PURPOSE IN LIFE and PERSONAL GROWTH (eudaimonic) DECLINE with age, while SWB/life satisfaction stays flat or rises - so the two move in opposite directions over the life course, proving they are not one thing
- Direction for calibration: treat Israel's profile as high-eudaimonic (meaning/purpose) with only mid hedonic rank - the split lets that co-exist with high TFR without requiring high life-satisfaction

## Caveats

- Digest-only: definitions and the divergence magnitudes are grounded in the review's cited sources, not lifted verbatim from the paywalled text; the ~0.4‒0.8 correlation band is the range reported across the studies Ryan and Deci review, not a single headline coefficient
- The paper is a review, not a fertility study - it supplies the CONSTRUCT SEPARATION only; the meaning-to-fertility link comes from Nelson et al. 2014 and the Israel digests, not here
- Hedonic and eudaimonic measures overlap enough that some scholars treat well-being as one super-factor; E42 leans on the separability finding, which is contested but well-supported by the age-dissociation result
