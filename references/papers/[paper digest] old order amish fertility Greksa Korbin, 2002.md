# [Paper digest] Population Growth and Fertility Patterns in an Old Order Amish Settlement

**Authors**: Lawrence P. Greksa, Jill E. Korbin<br>
**Year**: 2002  **Venue**: Annals of Human Biology 29(2): 192-201<br>
**Original (source)**: [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03014460110075684](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03014460110075684) (DOI 10.1080/03014460110075684)<br>

**Grounds**: E17-H135 (high-fertility subculture as an existence proof - but heavily confounded)

## Key mechanism

A closed, pronatalist religious community sustains natural (uncontrolled) fertility: no contraception, early and near-universal marriage, strong communal and doctrinal support for large families, and high retention of the young into the church. Population growth is therefore driven by very high completed fertility combined with low defection - not by any transferable policy instrument.

## Main findings

- Geauga Settlement, northeastern Ohio; directory data on 1,337 families through 1 Jan 1993
- Completed marital fertility ~7.7 births per woman in the 2002 study - a natural-fertility population (the ~6.9 figure in the task brief is from the authors' later 21-year follow-up, showing modest decline)
- Population roughly doubles about every 20 years
- High retention of Amish-born youth into the church (commonly cited ~85%+, defection ~15%), the second engine alongside fertility

## Key takeaways

- Existence proof that sustained above-replacement fertility is possible inside a modern high-income country - fertility is not biologically capped low
- But confounded by endogamy, community closure, and religious selection: the "mechanism" is a total subculture, not a policy lever a state can copy
- The transferable signal is weak - retention + pronatalist norms + universal early marriage as a bundle, not any single reproducible intervention
