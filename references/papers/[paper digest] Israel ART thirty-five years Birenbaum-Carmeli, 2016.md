## Key mechanism

Documents the world's most generous IVF entitlement and asks what it buys demographically. Israel funds fertility treatment as a near-unconditional right, embedded in a pronatalist state culture. The mechanism is a genuine STRUCTURAL lever - it removes the price of ART at the point of use - but its direct contribution to the TFR is small in level terms (a few percent of births); its larger role is symbolic, reinforcing the pronatal norm that "everyone should have children," including single women and same-sex couples.

## Main findings

- Ministry of Health entitles every Israeli woman aged 18 to 45, irrespective of family status or sexual orientation, to unlimited funded treatment up to the birth of two live children with her current partner
- Per-capita usage is the world's highest: 5,311 IVF cycles per million people, vs Denmark 2,618 (2nd), vs EU average 1,151 - "over five times the European average and ten times the international average"; Israeli women undergo about twice as many cycles per capita as Danish women
- Annual cycles rose from ~5,000 (1990) to ~40,000 (2012); treatment-cycle rate rose 82.7% between 2000 and 2012
- IVF-assisted births grew from 1.7% of live births (1995) to 4.3% (2013) - a real but modest share of total fertility
- Cost ~2% of Israel's national health budget; Israel has the world's highest rate of IVF clinics per capita
- Context: out-of-wedlock births are rare (~5%); private add-ons (egg/sperm donation \$7,000‒8,000 per cycle, \$12,000 shared-risk) sit on top of the funded core

## Key takeaways

- The "5x OECD / 10x world" per-capita claim is verified from the source (5,311 vs 1,151 EU per million)
- Classification: STRUCTURAL-LEVER, transferable in form (any state can fund ART) but LOW direct fertility yield - it explains ~4% of births, not the ~1-child gap over peer countries; the norm it signals is the larger effect
- The policy is downstream of, and reinforces, the pronatal norm - it is an enabling channel, not the primary driver
