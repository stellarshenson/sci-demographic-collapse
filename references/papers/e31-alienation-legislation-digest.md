[paper digest] parental alienation - prevalence, legislation, prevention, and fertility (E31)

Research digest for batch H261-H270. Topic: parental alienation (PA), the measured effects of custody legislation and PA-prevention on contact, on fertility, and on protective parents. The science is genuinely contested; this digest represents both the pro-contact fertility evidence and the documented weaponisation side-effect faithfully, without laundering either.

## 1. Prevalence - real numbers, heavily contested measurement

- Bernet (2010) / Warshak (2015): an estimated ~1% of all children are alienated from a parent to some degree; ~3.8 million US children estimated moderately-to-severely alienated. This is the conservative clinical-scale figure
- Harman, Leder-Elder & Biringen (2016), representative US poll: 13.4% of parents report being alienated from at least one child, scaled to ~22.1 million US parents. Self-report, no clinical adjudication
- Harman, Leder-Elder & Biringen (2019): higher self-report estimates - ~35.5% (US) and ~32% (Canada) of parents report being targets of alienating behaviours. Larger numbers reflect a broad "alienating behaviours" definition, not diagnosed alienation
- Divorced vs intact: college-student sample, 29.4% from divorced families vs 12.9% from intact families reported being alienated from a parent - divorce roughly doubles reported prevalence
- CONTESTED measurement: estimates chain self-report onto assumptions about high-conflict-divorce counts; there is no diagnostic gold standard. PA was excluded from DSM-5 and the WHO decided in 2020 NOT to include "parental alienation" in ICD-11 because "it is not a health care term." Prevalence is therefore a range, advocacy-sensitive, and must be bounded not point-estimated

## 2. Legislation - measured effects

Shared / joint physical custody (the causal-fertility anchor):
- Halla (2013), JEEA 11(2):278-315 - US state joint-custody reforms: marital fertility rose ~8% over ~18 years, up to ~14% in the longer run, strongest for women aged 35-44; marriage rate +~5% (appearing after a ~5-year lag); female labour-force participation fell; male suicide and domestic violence fell. Mechanism: joint custody raised men's within-marriage bargaining power (marriage/childbearing more attractive to men). This is THE one clean causal custody-to-fertility estimate
- Fernandez-Kranz, Nollenberger & Roff (2024, American Journal of Health Economics; IZA DP 13810): Spain's joint-physical-custody (EPT) reform raised the JPC share from <11% to ~40% of divorces in five years and reduced intimate-partner violence by ~45%, with reduced female-partner homicides in treated regions. Same bargaining mechanism (father's outside option improves). No direct fertility estimate, but confirms the pro-contact mechanism is not DV-increasing in the Spanish design
- Kentucky HB 528 (2018), first statewide rebuttable 50/50 presumption: divorce rate fell 25% (2016-2023) vs 18% nationally - BUT Stone (2023) shows a pre-existing downward trend, matched by neighbour states and unchanged in slope after 2018, i.e. the reduction is not credibly caused by the law. Fertility effect unmeasured. A domestic-violence order auto-rebuts the presumption, yet advocates report 50/50 awarded despite documented abuse
- Arizona (2013 statute directing courts to "maximize" parenting time), evaluated ~4 years post (Votruba/Braver et al., 2018): functions as a de-facto rebuttable equal-time presumption; rated positive overall and for children's best interests; NEUTRAL effect on legal and interparental conflict; SMALL increases in allegations of domestic violence, child abuse, and substance abuse. No fertility measure

Anti-alienation / criminalisation (the backfire case):
- Brazil Lei 12.318/2010 - the world's only statute defining and penalising "acts of parental alienation." Law 14,340/2022 (signed 2022) amended it, removing suspension of parental authority from the available measures. OHCHR (2022, 2025) and the UN Special Rapporteur (Alsalem) have called for full repeal; Brazil's Chamber of Deputies CCJ approved a revocation proposal (Bills 2812/2022, 1372/2023). A 2026 legal review documents negative impacts on custody decision-making, children's best interests, and women's rights: fathers accused of domestic or sexual abuse reframe mothers as "alienators," weaponising the law against protections under Lei Maria da Penha. NO positive fertility or child-welfare outcome has been measured
- Fines / sanctions / make-up time / contempt for access denial: only qualitative legal-practice literature. Civil contempt is coercive (make-up time, fines, attorney fees, jail as last resort). NO population-scale evidence that it durably increases contact or is cost-neutral; it raises adversarial conflict. Effect must be bounded as small and uncertain, with a conflict-escalation cost

## 3. Prevention vectors

- Reunification therapy / camps: efficacy claims are weak and the ethics/DV backlash is strong. Kayden's Law (federal, VAWA 2022 reauthorisation - Keeping Children Safe from Family Violence Act) incentivises states to require scientific proof of program safety and to ban therapies that cut a child off from a safe parent. State bans/limits followed - Colorado (first compliant statute), California ("Piqui's Law"), Arizona, Utah, Pennsylvania, Tennessee ("Abrial's Law," 2024). Contested efficacy plus a documented due-process / child-welfare cost when applied against a safe parent
- Co-parenting / divorce education mandates: Guyette (2024, Family Court Review) and earlier reviews (Sigal, Sandler et al., 2011) find MIXED evidence - only a few studies show lower relitigation, with methodological limits, and no convincing evidence of durable interparental-conflict reduction; some pre-post and RCT studies do show modest gains in parenting, conflict and child behaviour. Cheap, low-harm, modest
- Early mediation (diverting contested custody from litigation): Emery/Dillon RCT with 12-year follow-up (Emery et al., 2001) - at 12 years, 52% of nonresident parents who mediated talked with their child weekly vs 14% of those who litigated; more nonresident involvement across domains, with NO associated increase in coparenting conflict. Strong contact-preservation signal that feeds the intergenerational dividend
- Relationship-skills / voluntary couples therapy: the E21 null holds - population RCTs (Building Strong Families, Supporting Healthy Marriage) were null-with-fade; PREP Strong Bonds (Stanley 2010) army-divorce RCT. Voluntary marriageability (fq) pushes fade

## 4. Side-effects - the contested science and the weaponisation cost

- PA is NOT in DSM-5; WHO did not include it in ICD-11 (2020), stating it "is not a health care term"
- UN Special Rapporteur Reem Alsalem (2023), A/HRC/53/36 "Custody, violence against women and violence against children": characterises parental alienation as an "unscientific pseudo-concept" used in custody courts to discredit abuse-alleging mothers
- Meier, Dickson, O'Sullivan, Rosen & Hayes (2020), Journal of Social Welfare and Family Law 42(1), NIJ-funded, 4,338 US custody cases (2005-2014): mothers' abuse claims - especially child physical/sexual abuse - increase their risk of losing custody, and fathers' cross-claims of alienation roughly DOUBLE that risk. Gender-specific: fathers alleging abuse are not similarly undermined by a mother's alienation cross-claim. This is the documented weaponisation mechanism against protective parents
- Implication for the model: any anti-alienation lever carries a DV-overlap due-process cost that must be stacked honestly; the same enforcement machinery that preserves benign father contact also hands an abuser a custody weapon

## 5. Intergenerational path integral - sign and selection discount

- McLanahan, Tach & Schneider (2013), Annual Review of Sociology 39:399-427, "The Causal Effects of Father Absence": father absence has robust effects on social-emotional outcomes across the life course, BUT selection plays a real role - effects are partly causal, partly confounded by who separates. So the delayed dividend from contact-preserving legislation must be SELECTION-DISCOUNTED, not taken at face value
- Sign rule for the model: contact-preserving legislation (shared custody, paternity certainty, early mediation) feeds a POSITIVE delayed dividend (fF+, next-cohort marriageability q rises on a 27-45yr distributed lag, compounding). Conflict-increasing legislation (criminalisation, forced reunification, contempt wars) feeds a NEGATIVE delayed scar (fScar+, next-cohort q falls). Same legislative domain, opposite sign, decided by whether the lever lowers or raises inter-parental conflict and whether it severs or preserves a safe-parent bond
