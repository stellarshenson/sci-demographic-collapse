## The argument (framing, not an operator)

Challenges the demographic-transition assumption that fertility decline is irreversible. From an evolutionary standpoint irreversibility is suspect because demographic traits are partly heritable and responsive to conditions. Offers propositions for how fertility could rise again:

- **Natural/cultural selection on high-fertility variants** - the heritable-fertility rebound (same mechanism as Collins-Page 2019, Kolk 2014)
- **Difficulty of maintaining universal low-fertility norms** in a large, diverse, economically differentiated population - i.e. norm fidelity is imperfect and heterogeneous, so pronatal pockets can re-expand
- **Escalating resource demands of modernization** as an ecological feedback on fertility

## How differential fertility enters

As the engine of the rebound: sub-populations with higher fertility (and any heritable/cultural component) gain share over generations. Burger-DeLong is the qualitative case *for* building selection-on-fertility into projections; Arenberg et al. (2021) is the quantitative caution that the rebound needs `p·F > 1`.

## Portability to our ensemble

Motivational: justifies giving our bearer a genuine selection dynamic (not a fixed TFR), while its "norms are hard to keep universal" point argues for **heterogeneous fidelity across the ensemble** - different OT particles/cohorts carrying different `φ_c`, so the leak is emergent from dispersion rather than a single global constant.
