## Summary

Evidence on the effect of Norway's non-transferable "father's quota" of parental leave introduced in 1993.

## Key findings

- Paternity-leave uptake rose from under 3% (pre-1993) to ~85% within a few years, ~97% by 2008
- The quota is non-transferable use-it-or-lose-it - fathers cluster exactly at the quota (default anchoring)
- Where quotas were removed (Denmark episodes), uptake fell; reinstating them raised it again
- Voluntary/opt-in equivalents produced near-zero take-up (free-riding)

## Relevance to this project

The canonical proof of the E16 design axiom (H121): a mandatory, non-transferable default has δ≈0 and durable uptake, while opt-in equivalents collapse to free-riding - the reason enforceable structural levers dominate exhortation in the defection screen.
