# [Paper digest] Declines in Sexual Frequency among American Adults, 1989-2014

**Grounds**: E17-H134 (the "sex recession" is partnership decline, not porn)

## Key mechanism

Aggregate sexual frequency fell because fewer adults are partnered, not because partnered adults lost interest to substitutes. The decline decomposes into (1) a compositional shift - more people without a steady partner - and (2) a within-couple drop concentrated among the married/cohabiting. Younger cohorts (millennials, iGen) are less likely to have a steady partner, so partnership formation, the upstream input to fertility, is the binding constraint.

## Main findings

- American adults had sex ~9 fewer times per year in 2010-2014 than in 1995-1999 (GSS, N ~26,000)
- Married / cohabiting couples had sex ~16 fewer times per year in 2010-2014 vs 2000-2004 - the sharpest drop
- Reversal: in the 1990s married people out-had never-married people; by the mid-2000s the never-married had more sex, because fewer young adults are partnered at all
- Decline held across region, education, race, and work hours; not explained by longer working hours or by pornography

## Key takeaways

- The "sex recession" is a partnership-formation problem: the missing input is steady unions, upstream of any within-couple frequency story
- Undercuts the porn-as-cause narrative for declining sex - the drop is compositional (fewer couples) plus a married-couple frequency fall
- Reinforces union-duration / partnership as the lever, consistent with the library's union-instability and dating-market digests
