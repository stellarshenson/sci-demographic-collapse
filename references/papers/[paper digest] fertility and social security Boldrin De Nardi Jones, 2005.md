# [Paper digest] Fertility and Social Security

**Authors**: Michele Boldrin, Mariacristina De Nardi, Larry E. Jones<br>
**Year**: 2005  **Venue**: NBER Working Paper w11146 (later J. Demographic Economics)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11146/w11146.pdf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11146/w11146.pdf)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] fertility and social security Boldrin De Nardi Jones, 2005.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E18-H152 (PAYG pension externality on fertility)

## Summary

Public pay-as-you-go (PAYG) social security substitutes for children as an old-age support asset, so a larger pension system crowds out the private incentive to have children. The paper calibrates this channel against the 20th-century fertility decline and the Europe-US fertility gap.

## Key findings

- Cross-country regressions: moving social security from 0% to 10% of GNP is associated with a fall of 0.7 to 1.6 children per woman (TFR); Regression II with added controls gives 1.0 to 1.8
- The old-age-security (Boldrin-Jones/Caldwell) model, unlike Barro-Becker altruism, produces a sizeable effect and accounts for 55-65% of the observed Europe-US fertility differences
- Access to capital markets (an alternative to children as a savings vehicle) explains much of the remaining decline
- Mechanism: children are a return-bearing asset; state pensions socialise old-age support and remove that private return

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H152: a PAYG pension system is a structural, often-ignored anti-natal force operating through the old-age-support motive. The calibrated magnitude (pension ~10% of GDP linked to -0.7..-1.6 TFR) makes it a first-order undercurrent, not a footnote - pro-natal cash transfers fight a headwind the pension system itself creates.
