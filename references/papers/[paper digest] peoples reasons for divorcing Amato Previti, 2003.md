**Original (source)**: [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0192513X03254507](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0192513X03254507) (DOI 10.1177/0192513X03254507)<br>

**Grounds**: E17-H131 / E17-H132 (what actually ends marriages - the interpersonal, not the structural)

## Key mechanism

Coding people's own open-ended accounts of why their marriage ended reveals that the proximate causes are interpersonal breaches (infidelity, incompatibility, substance abuse) rather than economic shocks. This anchors which levers plausibly move dissolution: the exit is triggered by relational failures, so exit access (H131) and relationship / conflict skills (H132) target the real triggers.

## Main findings

- 208 divorced individuals from national panel data (Marital Instability Over the Life Course, 1980-1997)
- Infidelity was the single most-cited reason (~21.6%), then incompatibility (~19.2%), drinking / drug use (~10.6%), and growing apart
- Gendered pattern: women more often cited their partner's infidelity, substance abuse, and abuse; higher-SES and women reported more relationship-centred (vs situational) reasons
- People blaming their partner or citing infidelity showed poorer post-divorce adjustment

## Key takeaways

- The triggers are relational, not primarily financial - money issues (Dew 2012) matter as a corrosive disagreement domain, but the named breaking points are infidelity and incompatibility
- Justifies pairing an exit valve (H131) with skills-based prevention (H132): the modifiable causes are behavioural and interpersonal
- Perceptions are gendered - a caution for one-size levers
