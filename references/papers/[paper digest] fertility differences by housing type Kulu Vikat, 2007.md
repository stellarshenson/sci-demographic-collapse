# [Paper digest] Fertility Differences by Housing Type: The Effect of Housing Conditions or of Selective Moves?

**Authors**: Hill Kulu, Andres Vikat<br>
**Year**: 2007  **Venue**: Demographic Research 17(26):775-802 (open access)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol17/26/17-26.pdf](https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol17/26/17-26.pdf)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] fertility differences by housing type Kulu Vikat, 2007.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E18-H161 (housing space raises fertility), E18-H147 (family/single-family housing)

## Summary

Uses Finnish register data to separate two explanations for why fertility varies by dwelling type: housing conditions actually shaping childbearing, versus selective moves (couples who plan a child relocate to larger homes first). Event-history models with anticipatory-move controls.

## Key findings

- Fertility is highest for couples in single-family houses and lowest for those in apartments
- Part of the gap is anticipatory selection - couples move to a house shortly before a birth
- A significant housing-conditions effect remains after controlling for selective moves: space itself supports higher fertility
- Fertility rises after a move to a larger dwelling, linking residential relocation to childbearing decisions

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H161 and H147: living space is a causal input to fertility, not merely a marker of couples who already intend children. The policy implication is that expanding family-suitable housing stock (single-family / larger units) is a supply-side lever on births, distinct from a housing-price subsidy - consistent with the Dettling-Kearney finding that the real housing lever is supply, not price transfers.
