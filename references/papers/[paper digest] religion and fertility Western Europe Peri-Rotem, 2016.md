# [Paper digest] Religion and Fertility in Western Europe

**Author**: Nitzan Peri-Rotem (University of Cambridge, Dept. of Sociology)<br>
**Year**: 2016  **Venue**: European Journal of Population 32(2):231‒265, DOI 10.1007/s10680-015-9371-z (open access, CC-BY)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4875064/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4875064/) (open access, PMC4875064)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] religion and fertility Western Europe Peri-Rotem, 2016.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E42 (eudaimonia-vs-hedonia arm - separates the MEANING/religiosity component of fertility from the E33 attendance/observance channels already modelled)

## Key mechanism

Peri-Rotem shows that in secular Western Europe religious PRACTICE still predicts higher fertility across cohorts, and - crucially for E42 - the gap is between the actively PRACTICING and everyone else, holding denomination constant. This lets the project separate the "meaning/commitment" component of religiosity (being a practicing believer for whom faith supplies purpose) from bare affiliation and from the observance/norm channels E33 already encodes. Where secularisation theory predicts the religion-fertility link should vanish, she finds it PERSISTS or WIDENS in France and the Netherlands - practicing women hold high or rising fertility while nominal and non-affiliated women decline. The transferable ingredient for E42 is that active religious commitment (a meaning/purpose source) carries a fertility premium that survives controls for affiliation, education and period - i.e. it is the meaning dimension, not the label.

## Main findings

- Practicing religious women maintain high or RISING completed fertility across cohorts, while nominal and non-affiliated women decline - the fertility gap by religiosity is stable or WIDENING in France and the Netherlands, and only narrows in Britain
- The active/nominal divide is the operative cut, not affiliation alone: within each denomination the more devout (by attendance and importance of religion) have larger families
- Secularisation paradigm REFUTED for individual behaviour: religious affiliation and practice "continue to be important determinants of fertility and family formation" despite institutional religious decline
- Longitudinal (event-history) analysis confirms religious adherence predicts SUBSEQUENT childbearing, not just cross-sectional correlation - the effect is on the transition to (and progression beyond) first birth

## Parameters for the model

- **Religiosity cut point**: monthly service attendance is the practicing/nominal threshold used (attend "once a month or more" = practicing; less/never = nominal); weekly attenders are only ~9‒10% of population in all three countries (2008‒2009), too small to split separately
- **Completed-fertility differentials (illustrative cohort numbers)**:
  - France, practicing Catholic: rebound to ~2.6 children vs nominal Catholic ~1.9 and non-affiliated ~1.7 in the same younger cohorts - a practicing-vs-secular gap of ~0.7‒0.9 child
  - Netherlands, practicing conservative Protestant: high, declining 3.5 -> 2.6; nominal counterparts fell toward ~2.2; practicing Catholic ~2.9 declining but staying above nominal
  - General pattern: practicing women hold ~2.5‒2.6+; nominal / non-affiliated fall to ~1.7‒1.9 - a persistent ~0.5‒0.9 child premium attributable to active commitment
- **Method**: retrospective birth histories, Generations and Gender Programme + British Household Panel Survey; discrete-time hazard (logistic) model for transition to first birth, estimated separately for pre-1960 and post-1960 birth cohorts, controlling for education and cohort
- **Model use**: attribute the ~0.5‒0.9 child practicing-vs-secular gap to the MEANING/commitment sub-channel and hold it distinct from the E33 observance-norm and community channels - so the religiosity effect in the model is not double-counted, and the meaning component can be dialed independently of pure attendance

## Caveats

- Western-European Christian samples (Britain, France, Netherlands) - the practicing/nominal gap is not directly the Israeli-Jewish gradient (Okun 2017 covers that); use for the MEANING-vs-observance DECOMPOSITION, not for Israel's magnitude
- The fertility numbers are read from cohort trend figures (Figs. 3a‒c), so treat them as illustrative ranges, not point estimates with published CIs
- Practice correlates with selection (committed people differ in many ways); the paper controls education and cohort but cannot fully rule out that the practicing are pre-selected on family orientation - the meaning premium is an upper bound
