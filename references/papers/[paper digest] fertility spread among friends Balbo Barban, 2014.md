# [Paper digest] Does Fertility Behavior Spread Among Friends?

**Authors**: Nicoletta Balbo, Nicola Barban<br>
**Year**: 2014  **Venue**: American Sociological Review 79(3)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122414531596](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122414531596)<br>
**Local PDF**: not downloaded - original is paywalled or render-blocked; use the link above<br>
**Used in**: E15-H84 (seed fertility social contagion in peer networks)

## Summary

Panel analysis of whether a friend's childbearing raises an individual's own transition to parenthood.

## Key findings

- Net of confounders, a friend's childbearing increases one's own risk of becoming a parent
- The contagion effect peaks 12-24 months after the friend's birth and fades to negligible after ~3 years
- Similar peer effects appear among siblings and coworkers

Grounds H84: childbearing is socially contagious, so seeding parenthood through real peer networks spreads it (a timing/norm lever). PDF paywalled (SAGE) - link to the article.
