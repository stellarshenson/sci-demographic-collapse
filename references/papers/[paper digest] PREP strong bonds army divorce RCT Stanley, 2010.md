# [Paper digest] Decreasing Divorce in U.S. Army Couples: A Randomized Controlled Trial Using PREP for Strong Bonds

**Authors**: Scott M. Stanley, Elizabeth S. Allen, Howard J. Markman, Galena K. Rhoades, Donnella Prentice<br>
**Year**: 2010  **Venue**: Journal of Couple & Relationship Therapy 9(2): 149-160 (PMC2902195)<br>
**Original (source)**: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2902195/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2902195/)<br>
**Local PDF**: not downloaded - NIH author manuscript, not in the PMC open-access subset (readable at the link); digest-only<br>
**Grounds**: E17-H132 (relationship-skills education as a divorce-prevention lever)

## Key mechanism

Structured couple relationship education (PREP - the Prevention and Relationship Enhancement Program) teaches communication and conflict-management skills before distress hardens. Delivered as a fielded program by Army chaplains at scale, it tests whether skills training moves the hard outcome - divorce - not just self-reported satisfaction.

## Main findings

- Randomized trial of 476 married Army couples: intervention n = 248 (246 analyzed), no-treatment control n = 228 (226 analyzed)
- One year on, the PREP group divorced at 2.03% (5/246) vs 6.20% (14/226) in controls - about one-third the rate (chi-square = 4.26, p < .05)
- The 2014 follow-up (Family Relations, PMC4237282, already in this library) found no enduring average effect at 2 years, with divorce prevention concentrated in higher-risk couples

## Key takeaways

- Relationship education can cut short-run divorce sharply in a fielded, scalable delivery (chaplains), not just in the lab
- Paired with the 2014 result, the honest read is a targeted lever: strong early / high-risk effect that does not persist as a universal average - dosage and targeting matter
- Note: the task brief cites "Journal of Family Psychology"; the RCT reporting the 2.03% vs 6.20% divorce split is this JCRT 2010 paper
