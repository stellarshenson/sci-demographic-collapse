## Key mechanism

Sobotka and Beaujouan document that, across Europe and three decades, the DECLARED ideal family size has barely moved and stays anchored at two children, even as realised (period) fertility has fallen well below two. The two-child norm behaves like a stable, near-universal cultural attractor that does not track the downward drift in births, opening a persistent GAP between what people say is ideal (~2.2) and what they do (TFR often ~1.3‒1.6). This matters because a stable-but-unrealised ideal is latent demand - potential fertility held down by constraints and by the stated-vs-behaviour divergence, and (paired with Kuran) a reservoir of preference that could cascade if conditions shifted.

## Main findings

- The mean personal ideal family size is closely clustered around 2.2 in most European countries and has been remarkably stable across the study period
- About six in ten women (~60%) state exactly TWO children as their personal ideal - a proportion that is very similar across European regions
- Countries that historically reported HIGHER ideals have converged downward over time toward the two-child model, so cross-country dispersion in ideals has shrunk
- In the lowest-fertility countries (completed cohort fertility ~1.6 or below), the mean ideal has slipped to a still-near-replacement ~1.95‒2.15 - a modest erosion, not a collapse of the ideal
- The persistence is most striking in GERMAN-SPEAKING countries (Austria, western and eastern Germany, Switzerland), where ideals stay near replacement despite very low actual fertility and a comparatively larger minority endorsing fewer than two children
- The realised-vs-ideal gap is therefore large and durable: the ideal stays ~2.2 while period TFR sits well under 2 across most of the continent

## Method and identification

- Data: responses of women of reproductive age pooled from 168 surveys across 37 European countries/populations, spanning 1979‒2012 (Eurobarometer, ISSP, Generations and Gender, and national surveys)
- Descriptive/comparative design: tracks the distribution and mean of stated personal ideal family size over time and across regions, alongside realised fertility
- Identification is trend-and-convergence, not causal: the finding is the STABILITY and clustering of the ideal against a falling fertility baseline, robust across many independent surveys

## Key takeaways for the model

- Set the declared-ideal anchor at ~2.2 (with ~60% naming two) and treat the ideal MINUS realised TFR as the campaign's latent-demand / unrealised-fertility gap (H372) - the norm is not the binding constraint, the realisation is
- The ideal is a sticky cultural attractor near replacement: a low-fertility equilibrium can persist WITHOUT the norm having fallen, so a model that reads collapse off stated preferences would mis-predict - the gap, not the ideal, is the state variable
- The German-speaking exception (ideal stays ~2, fertility very low, larger 0‒1-child minority) is the cleanest case of a wide, durable ideal-realisation gap; couple with Kuran's preference-falsification to test whether a stable-but-unmet ideal is cascade-primed or just chronically frustrated
