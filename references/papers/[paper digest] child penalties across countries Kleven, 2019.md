# [Paper digest] Child Penalties Across Countries: Evidence and Explanations

**Authors**: Henrik Kleven, Camille Landais, Johanna Posch, Andreas Steinhauer, Josef Zweimuller<br>
**Year**: 2019  **Venue**: NBER Working Paper 25524 (AEA P&P)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25524/w25524.pdf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25524/w25524.pdf)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] child penalties across countries Kleven, 2019.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E15-H62

## Summary

Comparative event-study estimates of the long-run earnings penalty of motherhood across six countries.

## Key findings

- Men and women follow parallel earnings paths until the first birth, then diverge sharply
- The long-run penalty is large and persistent and varies widely by country/policy regime
- Gender norms explain much of the cross-country variation

## Relevance to this project

Companion to the Atlas; the earlier cross-country evidence behind the child-penalty channel used in H62.
