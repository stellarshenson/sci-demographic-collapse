## Key mechanism

This is the linchpin for E42. Nelson et al. review the whole "do children make parents happy?" literature and reframe the question: the parent-happiness link is mixed and often null or negative, but parenthood reliably raises MEANING and PURPOSE. Their model (Figure 1) routes parenthood to well-being through competing mediators - POSITIVE paths (purpose/meaning in life, satisfaction of basic human needs, positive emotions, enhanced social roles) and NEGATIVE paths (negative emotions, financial strain, sleep disturbance, strained partner relationships). The eudaimonic path (meaning) is where children pay off; the hedonic path (moment-to-moment happiness) is where they cost. This is exactly the split the round posits: purpose loads positive, hedonic satisfaction does not - and it is measured on parenthood itself, the fertility-relevant behaviour.

## Main findings

- The parenthood-happiness relationship is genuinely MIXED across three method families (parent-vs-nonparent comparisons, transition-to-parenthood panels, day-reconstruction/experience-sampling); no clean positive hedonic effect
- Parenthood is far more consistently linked to MEANING and PURPOSE than to happiness - children reliably raise the sense that life is significant even when they lower day-to-day mood
- The negative hedonic component is concentrated and conditional: parents suffer to the extent they hit greater negative emotion, magnified FINANCIAL strain, SLEEP disturbance, and troubled marriages - all moderators, not universals
- Fathers with children in the home report GREATER positive emotion than nonparents (Umberson & Gove) - the hedonic penalty is not uniform and is smaller/absent for fathers
- Moderators decide the sign: parent age (older parents fare better), whether children live in the home, marital status, and whether the child was planned - so "parenthood" has no single well-being coefficient

## Parameters for the model

- **Two-channel structure (primary usable parameter)**: parenthood -> well-being splits into a EUDAIMONIC channel (purpose/meaning, needs, social roles: POSITIVE) and a HEDONIC channel (mood net of financial strain + sleep loss + partner strain: NEAR-ZERO or NEGATIVE) - wire meaning as the positive fertility-supporting term, hedonic satisfaction as non-loading
- **Meaning measured as single items** in the underpinning studies: World Values Survey "How often do you think about the meaning and purpose of life?" (1 = often to 4 = never) and experience-sampling "how personally significant and meaningful is what you are doing?" (1‒7) - cheap, one-item proxies the project can mirror
- **Negative hedonic mediators are the levers, not parenthood per se**: financial strain, sleep disturbance, and partner-relationship strain are the three named cost channels - these connect E42 to the existing affordability / second-shift / partner-quality levers already in the model
- **Sign asymmetry by gender**: fathers-in-home show a positive affect gap vs nonparents; do not assume a symmetric hedonic penalty across sexes

## Caveats

- Correlational/review evidence - the model organises effects, it does not establish that raising population meaning would causally raise TFR; it establishes that the meaning channel is where parenthood's well-being payoff lives
- Nearly all reviewed data are Western (US-heavy); the meaning-payoff-of-children pattern is not separately validated for Israel here - it is the mechanism, the Israel digests supply the high-meaning-high-TFR anchor
- Meaning is measured with single items in the source studies; construct precision is weaker than a full MLQ (see Steger 2006 digest)
