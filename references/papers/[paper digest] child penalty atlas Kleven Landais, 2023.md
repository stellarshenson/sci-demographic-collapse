# [Paper digest] The Child Penalty Atlas

**Authors**: Henrik Kleven, Camille Landais, and collaborators<br>
**Year**: 2023  **Venue**: NBER Working Paper 31649 (Review of Economic Studies 2025)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31649/w31649.pdf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31649/w31649.pdf)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] child penalty atlas Kleven Landais, 2023.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E15-H62 (early-parenthood security + job guarantee)

## Summary

Event-study child-penalty estimates on administrative registers for over 100 countries.

## Key findings

- Women's earnings fall sharply at the first birth and diverge permanently from men's
- The penalty persists at large magnitude even 20 years after the first child
- Cross-country range ~21% (Denmark) to ~61% (Germany); ~31% in the US

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H62: a state early-parenthood security + job guarantee caps exactly this child-penalty downside, which is the documented driver of postponement - a risk cap, distinct from cash (H51).
