## Key mechanism

Kuran's engine is PREFERENCE FALSIFICATION: under social or political pressure people misrepresent their private preferences in public, so expressed opinion can diverge enormously from privately held opinion. Each person carries a REVOLUTIONARY THRESHOLD - the level of visible public opposition at which they would stop faking and openly join. Society's outcome is the fixed point of the whole threshold distribution. Because thresholds are hidden, the public equilibrium looks stable yet can be latently fragile: a small trigger that pushes a few low-threshold individuals into the open lowers the effective threshold for the next tier, whose defection lowers it again - a self-reinforcing BANDWAGON that flips the system discontinuously from quiescence to mass revolt. The corollary is unpredictability: since preferences and thresholds are unobservable, neither observers nor participants can foresee the cascade - "unthinkable" until it becomes "inevitable".

## Main findings

- Public preference ≠ private preference: falsification lets a widely-disliked status quo persist because everyone underestimates how many others privately oppose it (pluralistic ignorance)
- Revolt is a threshold-cascade: mobilisation jumps when a trigger shifts the distribution enough to start a chain of defections; the same society can support both a "no revolt" and a "mass revolt" equilibrium
- The 1989 collapse illustrates the discontinuity - the Leipzig Monday demonstrations escalated to roughly 70,000 on 9 October 1989 and to several hundred thousand within weeks, and the Berlin Wall fell on 9 November 1989, a cascade compressed into about two months
- Inherent UNPREDICTABILITY: because the causal variables are hidden, even a complete model cannot forecast the timing - surprise is structural, not a failure of analysis
- Retrospective "inevitability" is an illusion: the pre-cascade equilibrium was genuinely stable

## Method and identification

- Formal threshold model of collective action (each agent a threshold on observed public opposition) combined with a historical case study of 1989 Eastern Europe
- Identifying claim is REGIME STABILITY vs FRAGILITY: multiple equilibria mean stability is not consent, so no cross-sectional statistic reveals how close a system sits to its tipping point
- Largely theoretical/historical - deliberately few statistics, since the decisive quantities (private preferences, thresholds) are unmeasured

## Key takeaways for the model

- Provides the cascade mechanic for the declared-vs-realised fertility gap (H372): a stated ideal diverging from behaviour is a form of preference falsification, so a low-fertility norm equilibrium may be metastable - a small shift could trigger a threshold-cascade in family-size preferences
- Warns against reading stability as consent: an observed low-fertility "norm" can hide private ideals near the 2.2 Sobotka anchor held down by conformity - carry a hidden-preference layer distinct from the expressed norm
- Adds structural UNPREDICTABILITY to any tipping term: because thresholds are latent, the timing of a norm reversal is not forecastable from current aggregates - treat cascade timing as intrinsically uncertain, not a fitted constant
