# [Paper digest] House Prices and Birth Rates: The Impact of the Real Estate Market on the Decision to Have a Baby

**Authors**: Lisa J. Dettling, Melissa S. Kearney<br>
**Year**: 2011 (JPubE 2014)  **Venue**: NBER Working Paper w17485<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17485/w17485.pdf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17485/w17485.pdf)<br>

**Used in**: E16-H114 (housing lever is supply, not a price subsidy)

## Summary

Estimates the causal effect of local house-price changes on birth rates, separating owners (who gain equity) from non-owners (who face a higher cost of housing a child).

## Key findings

- A \$10k rise in MSA house prices raises births ~5% among home-owners (equity effect)
- The same rise lowers births ~2.4% among non-owners (cost effect)
- Net effect at mean ownership is small and positive (~+0.8%)
- The signs are opposite by tenure - a price rise transfers fertility from young renters to older owners

## Relevance to this project

The anchor for H114: a housing-price subsidy to owners is dominated because it hurts the marginal childbearers (young renters); the real lever is supply/zoning reform that cuts the price young renters face.
