## Summary

Documents the empirical fact the E41 backtest could not reproduce: after two decades of falling to "lowest-low" levels (period TFR <= 1.3), period fertility TURNED AROUND across the developed world in the 2000s. The number of countries below 1.3 fell from **21 in 2003 to 5 in 2008** (four of the five stragglers - Hong Kong, Korea, Singapore, Taiwan - in East Asia). The recovery was not confined to the lowest-fertility countries; it lifted the whole developed world. The paper decomposes the rise into a tempo (postponement-ending) component and a genuine QUANTUM rebound, and this is the reference for a quantum recuperation term: the recovery came disproportionately from rising fertility at OLDER ages (delayed cohorts recuperating), a level rebound that occurs WHILE the mean age at birth is still rising - exactly the E46 "quantum, not tempo" signature for Germany.

## Key mechanism

The lowest-low period TFR is a transitory trough, not a stable attractor. Rapid postponement in the 1990s depressed the period TFR far below cohort first-birth levels through tempo and parity-composition distortions; as postponement decelerates and the first postponing cohorts reach older ages, the delayed births RECUPERATE, and the period TFR rebounds toward the (higher) underlying quantum. Improving economic conditions and family policy add a second, quantum push on top of the mechanical tempo recovery.

## Main findings (numbers for calibration)

- **Reversal scale**: countries with period TFR < 1.3 fell **21 (2003) -> 5 (2008)**; the upturn affected the whole developed world, not just the trough countries
- **Spain**: TFR **1.16 (1998, record low) -> 1.46 (2007)**, a rise of **+0.20**; native-women TFR rose +0.17 (migration contributed only ~16% of the rise)
- **Italy**: TFR rose **+0.11** between 1996 (near its low) and 2004; ~two-thirds attributable to rising fertility plus population composition
- **Quantum swing (their cohort-postponement simulation)**: a decline in quantum of **~0.2-0.4 children** during the postponement phase, followed by "a rise in quantum that is nearly equally dramatic" as postponement continues - the level bounces back, it is not a permanent loss
- **Recuperation locus**: the TFR increases "have come from increasing fertility at older ages" - the recuperation of births delayed by younger cohorts (Lesthaeghe-Willems, Frejka-Sardon), strongest in Northern and Western Europe
- **Economic channel**: median elasticity of TFR to log-unemployment **-0.23** (halving unemployment ~ +0.16 TFR) - a genuine quantum push distinct from the tempo mechanism
- **Tempo-adjusted TFR is "M-shaped"**: the Bongaarts-Feeney adjustment first overestimates, then underestimates, then again overestimates quantum over a ~50-year transition; it "works on average" but "can be systematically misleading over periods lasting a decade or more" - so the observed decade-scale recovery is a real quantum feature the period tempo term alone mis-reads

## Key takeaways for the model

- The four E41 recoveries (Germany, Poland, Israel, Italy) are instances of a developed-world-wide turn-around; a core that can only decline is falsified against a documented, general phenomenon - this is the empirical warrant for DEF-5
- The recovery is a QUANTUM LEVEL rebound that occurs while MAC still rises, so the fix is a mean-reversion / recuperation term on the quantum, NOT a tempo oscillator (confirms E46-H428's demotion of the tempo route)
- Magnitudes: recoveries of **+0.11 to +0.20** over ~8-10 years are the target scale; the quantum swing back is **0.2-0.4 children**, matching the Sobotka-Zeman-Frejka RI ~0.65 recuperation fraction
- Caveat: part of the rise is a mechanical tempo/composition artefact of postponement ending (Sobotka-Lutz 2009), so a quantum recuperation term must not double-count the tempo factor already in the identity - it captures only the LEVEL rebound net of the `(1 - kBF*dtau)` term
