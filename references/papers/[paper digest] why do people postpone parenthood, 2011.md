**Year**: 2011  **Venue**: Human Reproduction Update 17(6)<br>

## Summary

Review of the drivers of first-birth postponement and the policy levers that could counter it.

## Key findings

- Postponement is driven by education and career establishment, economic uncertainty, partnership timing and housing
- A worry about financial security raises the probability of delaying a first birth substantially
- Effective counters target the opportunity cost and the sequencing of career and family, not just cash

## Relevance to this project

Grounds the resequencing and assortative-postponement hypotheses (H93/H94): the binding constraint is the 'career and security first' script, which pushes first birth past the fertile window.
