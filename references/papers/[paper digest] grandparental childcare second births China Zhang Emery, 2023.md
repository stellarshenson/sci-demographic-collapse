# [Paper digest] Grandparental childcare and second births in China

**Authors**: Jing Zhang, Tom Emery<br>
**Year**: 2023  **Venue**: PLOS ONE 18(6): e0286496 (open access, CC-BY)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286496](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286496)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] grandparental childcare second births China Zhang Emery, 2023.pdf`<br>
**Used in**: E46 - the kin-childcare -> next-birth effect size in a contemporary low-fertility setting

## Summary

Tests whether grandparental childcare for the first child speeds and raises the transition to a second birth in China, a low-fertility country (TFR ~1.6 or below since 1997) with intensive grandparent involvement. Uses discrete-time / proportional-hazard survival models on the China Family Panel Studies, with grandparental childcare for the first child as the key covariate.

## Key findings

- **The odds of having a second child are about four times higher for mothers who use grandparental childcare than for those who do not** - the headline effect size
- The hazard of a second birth rises with time since first birth and **peaks around 48 months**, then declines
- Working mothers transition to a second birth *faster* than non-working mothers (HR = 0.70 on "not working", i.e. employed mothers progress quicker), consistent with grandparental care resolving the work-family conflict that otherwise suppresses second births
- The effect operates on both the *timing* (faster progression) and the *quantum* (higher ultimate probability) of the second birth
- Maternal and paternal grandparents both contribute; availability of the grandparent, not just co-residence, drives the effect

## Relevance to this project

The strongest contemporary, low-fertility effect size for the kin-childcare mechanism: available grandparental care roughly quadruples the odds of a next birth. This is an *availability of help* effect operating on parity progression - it lowers the effective cost of an additional child. It is observational (grandparents who help may be selected), so the causal magnitude is an upper bound, but it converges with Kaptijn-Thomese (Netherlands, +31 percentage points on the next birth). For the model this is the empirical anchor for a kin-support term that reduces the security/cost drag on progression to the next parity, most plausibly wired through `S` (lowering suppression) with a secondary lift to coupling `C`.
