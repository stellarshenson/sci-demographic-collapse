# [Paper digest] Are Tinder and Dating Apps Changing Dating and Mating in the US?

**Authors**: Michael Rosenfeld, Reuben Thomas, Sonia Hausen<br>
**Year**: 2019  **Venue**: Stanford (chapter/working paper); HCMST data<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://web.stanford.edu/~mrosenfe/Rosenfeld_Tinder_and%20Dating_apps.pdf](https://web.stanford.edu/~mrosenfe/Rosenfeld_Tinder_and%20Dating_apps.pdf)<br>
**Local PDF**: `[paper] dating apps mating Rosenfeld, 2019.pdf` (in this folder)<br>
**Used in**: E15-H65, H66 (attention economy: optionality and disintermediation)

## Summary

Analysis of the How Couples Meet and Stay Together data on the rise of online/app-mediated dating.

## Key findings

- Online dating is now the #1 way US couples meet, displacing friends, family and community
- The pairing rate did not rise despite the huge increase in search volume
- Meeting mode has no meaningful effect on relationship quality or longevity
- A larger partner pool can produce optionality/paralysis rather than commitment

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H65 (optionality/commitment-phobia) and H66 (disintermediation): apps thinned the effective partner market, so app regulation is necessary but not sufficient - offline intermediaries must be rebuilt.
