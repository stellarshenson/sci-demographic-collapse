[paper digest] Israel's exceptional fertility - the mechanisms behind the only above-replacement OECD country (E37)

Research digest for batch E37. Topic: ISRAEL fanned out into the ~10 distinct MECHANISMS behind its sustained above-replacement fertility (national TFR ~2.85 in 2023, highest in the OECD by far), each mapped to a model channel and classified as a transferable STRUCTURAL policy lever, population COMPOSITION (a high-fertility subgroup, not copyable), or an INHERENT-CULTURAL factor that cannot be legislated. The honest throughline, stated up front: Israel's outlier status is NOT the Haredi tail. The load-bearing fact is that the ~75% of Jewish Israelis who are secular or traditional reproduce at or just below replacement (secular 1.98, traditional-not-religious 2.22, traditional-religious 2.80) - above every other developed secular society. That gap is a NORM (pronatal familism fused with nationalism), and the norm is largely INHERENT-CULTURAL; the copyable structural levers (IVF funding, childcare) are real but explain only a small slice of the ~1-child advantage over peer countries. Consistent with the campaign throughline - structure beats psychology, but here the decisive input is a historically specific culture, not a policy dial, so transferability is genuinely limited.

## NUMBERS (triangulated; source + year inline)

- National period TFR 2023: CBS 2.85, UN WPP 2024 revision 2.83, OECD/press ~2.9 - CBS and UN AGREE (no Poland/GUS-style conflict). 2022 = 2.89. Fertility began falling in 2018 (Weinreb, Taub 2024)
- Replacement level for Israel = TFR 2.08, not 2.1 (low mortality + low secondary sex ratio; Weinreb, Taub 2024)
- Sector TFR, Jewish women, 3-yr mean 2020‒2022 (Hleihel 2023, via Weinreb Table 1): Haredi 6.38, Religious/Dati 3.77, Traditional-religious 2.80, Traditional-not-religious 2.22, Not-religious/secular 1.98. Secular hovered 2.09‒2.26 across 2008‒2018 - the surprising fact (secular ~2.0, above every developed secular society) is VERIFIED
- CBS TFR by religion 2022: Jewish 3.03, Muslim 2.91, Christian 1.68, Druze 1.85 (Muslim fell from ~9.2 in 1965 to ~3.3 by mid-2010s to 2.91 in 2022 - convergence, not a separate boom)
- Population shares: Jews 74%, Muslims 18%, Christians 2%, Druze 1.6% (CBS 2019). Jewish self-ID 2022 (IDI): secular 45%, traditional/masorti 33%, dati 12%, Haredi 10% (Haredi counted demographically = 13.6% of total population, 2023, Israel Democracy Institute; projected 16% by 2030)
- IVF/ART: funded for every woman 18‒45 regardless of status/orientation, unlimited cycles up to 2 live births with current partner; 5,311 cycles per million people (world's highest) vs Denmark 2,618 vs EU average 1,151 → "over 5x the European average, 10x the international average"; ~2% of the national health budget; IVF = 4.3% of live births in 2013 (Birenbaum-Carmeli 2016)
- Mean age at first birth ~28.6, flat since 2015 (CBS/Weinreb) - younger than most of the OECD; mean age at childbearing (all births) ~30.7‒30.9, just below the OECD average of 30.9 in 2022 (OECD Family Database SF2.3). Non-marital births rare, ~5%
- Female labour-force participation ~60.9% (2023, ages 15+, World Bank/national estimate) - coexists with TFR 2.9; OECD flags low Arab-women and high Jewish-women participation
- BEST model "Israel" calibration: TFR 2023 = 2.85 (CBS≈UN WPP), mean age at childbearing ≈ 30.8, age at first birth ≈ 28.6

## Channel-mapping logic (how an Israel effect becomes a forcing, or is flagged as composition)

- The measured Israel-vs-OECD gap (~1 child) decomposes across the model's own channels: pronatal norm N, marriage/coupling C, parity Pbar, tempo tau (younger first birth), childlessness rho (low), community/meaning S. The analytic task is attribution, not a new primitive
- A BOUNDED high-fertility group (Haredi TFR 6.38; settlers TFR>5) is NOT a channel forcing - it is a distinct Leslie sub-population with lambda>1 plus retention/endogamy. Model it as compounding COMPOSITION with an apostasy/leakage delta, exactly as the E33 religion round handled Amish/Hutterite/Haredi. Do NOT copy it as an fN dial on a collapsing country
- The transferable residual is the SECULAR near-replacement fertility. Okun's identification (the religiosity gap survives controls for women's employment and education) says that residual is a NORM, not an income/childcare price - so a collapsing country cannot buy it with cash or clinics alone
- Attribution honesty: the norm is a specific religion-plus-nationalism blend (DellaPergola; Okun). Iran fell 6.6→1.35 as a theocracy and Muslim-Israeli TFR fell 9.2→2.9 - religiosity alone does not hold fertility. What holds Israel's is the collective-future norm gated by low existential security, which is not a legislative instrument

## MECHANISM 1 - Pronatal familism default norm (across ALL sectors)

- Number: secular Jewish TFR 1.98, traditional-not-religious 2.22 (Hleihel 2023 / Weinreb Table 1); >1/5 of secular respondents aged 25‒49 have 3+ children (Okun 2017)
- Source: Okun (2017), Eur J Population 33:475‒507; Weinreb (Taub 2024); Fogiel-Bijaoui on familism
- Channel: norm N (the bistable pronatal norm built in E25). Okun's employment-does-not-explain-it result identifies it as normative, not economic
- Classification: STRUCTURAL-LEVER in principle (it IS the N channel), INHERENT-CULTURAL in practice - the norm is a historically specific religion-nationalism-familism blend
- Transferability: LOW as a direct copy - you cannot legislate a familism default; a collapsing country can only nudge N via the cheap coupling levers already found (E20 cohabitation-recognition, anti-atomisation), not import Israel's norm wholesale

## MECHANISM 2 - Universal state-funded IVF/ART

- Number: 5,311 cycles/million (5x EU, 10x world); funded 18‒45, unlimited to 2 live births; 2% of health budget; IVF = 4.3% of births (Birenbaum-Carmeli 2016)
- Source: Birenbaum-Carmeli (2016), Reprod BioMed Soc Online 2:16‒23; Shalev & Sperling on policy
- Channel: childlessness rho (lowers involuntary childlessness) + tempo tau (rescues later-age births)
- Classification: STRUCTURAL-LEVER, cleanly transferable in form - any state can fund ART
- Transferability: HIGH in form but LOW in yield - it explains ~4% of births, not the 1-child gap; its larger effect is signalling the pronatal norm. A real but small lever for a collapsing country; do not overweight it

## MECHANISM 3 - Religiosity gradient / Haredi compounding subpopulation

- Number: Haredi TFR 6.38, stable ~7 since the 1980s; 13.6% of population (2023), projected 16% by 2030 (Weinreb; Israel Democracy Institute 2024)
- Source: Hleihel (2023); Weinreb (Taub 2024); IDI Statistical Report on Ultra-Orthodox Society 2024
- Channel: NOT a forcing - a Leslie sub-population with lambda>1, retention and endogamy
- Classification: COMPOSITION (subpopulation compounding), same treatment as E33 Amish/Hutterite/Haredi
- Transferability: NONE as a policy - it is a bounded high-fertility subculture, not a copyable lever; model as a compounding sub-population with an apostasy leakage delta

## MECHANISM 4 - Collective-future / security / existential-demography

- Number: Jewish TFR re-emerged ABOVE replacement in 2008 (unique in the developed world); settler TFR >5 (2010); BUT only ~7% of women / ~9% of men say security worries would make them want MORE children (DellaPergola 2020)
- Source: DellaPergola (2020), Fertility Prospects in Israel; Fargues on conflict-fertility; Okun on the "demographic threat"
- Channel: norm N + meaning/community S (a secular source of traditionalist, family-oriented commitment)
- Classification: INHERENT-CULTURAL - the nationalism-security-collective-future blend is historically specific
- Transferability: NONE directly - the DIRECT security effect is a minority channel (~7‒9%); most of the effect is the standing norm. A collapsing country cannot and should not manufacture existential threat

## MECHANISM 5 - Dual-earner compatibility (high female LFP AND high fertility)

- Number: female LFP ~60.9% (2023, ages 15+) coexists with TFR 2.9; women's employment is negatively related to fertility but does NOT explain the religiosity gap (Okun 2017)
- Source: OECD Israel labour notes (2024); Okun (2017); Myrskyla et al. (2011) gender-equity turn (already in library)
- Channel: child penalty / opportunity-cost damping - the developed-world work-fertility tradeoff does not bind
- Classification: PARTLY STRUCTURAL (childcare density, family support) but the coexistence is mostly enabled by the norm + kin - INHERENT-CULTURAL
- Transferability: PARTIAL - childcare density and second-shift sharing are copyable (consistent with the campaign's gender-equity-in-the-home finding), but the tradeoff only fully dissolves under the familism norm; cannot be bought by childcare alone

## MECHANISM 6 - Kin proximity / intergenerational support

- Number: NOT separately sourced with an Israel-specific figure (could not source a clean grandmother-proximity statistic for Israel); leans on the general grandmother-distance literature already in the library (Engelhardt et al. 2019)
- Source: Engelhardt (2019) grandmother geographic distance (library); Israel's small geography (~22,000 km2) is contextual, not a measured lever
- Channel: alloparenting / childcare cost via S and the child-penalty damping
- Classification: STRUCTURAL/INHERENT hybrid, LOW provenance for Israel specifically - flagged
- Transferability: WEAK claim - plausible but not quantified for Israel; do not assign a hard Israel number without a dedicated source

## MECHANISM 7 - Child-centric public sphere / low marginal social cost per child

- Number: qualitative - no hard number sourced; supported by the familism norm (Okun 2017) and the low non-marital-birth share (~5%) indicating child-rearing embedded in stable families
- Source: Okun (2017); Fogiel-Bijaoui (familism); Weinreb (Taub 2024)
- Channel: cost/meaning S - lower perceived social and psychological cost per additional child
- Classification: INHERENT-CULTURAL, qualitative
- Transferability: NONE directly - a downstream expression of the familism norm, not an independent lever

## MECHANISM 8 - Immigration / aliyah / population-as-national-project

- Number: FSU immigrants arrived well below replacement (~1.5) and their cohort fertility converged UPWARD toward the Israeli norm over time (Demographic Research 50:13, 2024) - exact convergence figure not extracted here; aliyah adds population directly but immigrant fertility assimilates to the norm
- Source: DellaPergola (2020) on population-as-national-project; Nahmias/FSU cohort-fertility literature
- Channel: COMPOSITION (adds people) + norm-assimilation (N pulling immigrants up)
- Classification: COMPOSITION plus norm effect - the interesting finding is assimilation UPWARD, the opposite of the usual immigrant-convergence-downward pattern
- Transferability: LOW - aliyah is Israel-specific; the general lesson (a strong host norm can raise immigrant fertility rather than immigrants lowering the mean) is the transferable insight, not the policy

## MECHANISM 9 - Near-universal, relatively early marriage and low non-marital birth

- Number: non-marital births ~5% (Birenbaum-Carmeli 2016; Weinreb 2024); marriage near-universal across sectors; age at first birth ~28.6 (younger than most OECD)
- Source: Weinreb (Taub 2024); Birenbaum-Carmeli (2016)
- Channel: marriage/coupling C (a channel the model already owns) + tempo tau
- Classification: COMPOSITION/STRUCTURAL via the marriage channel - largely an expression of the familism norm
- Transferability: PARTIAL - the marriage channel is modellable, but near-universal marriage is itself norm-driven; consistent with the campaign's marriageable-men finding that births track the coupling channel

## MECHANISM 10 - Younger tempo and low childlessness (quantum backstop)

- Number: age at first birth ~28.6 flat since 2015 (vs OECD MAC 30.9); low childlessness relative to the OECD, reinforced by universal IVF (rho low)
- Source: Weinreb (Taub 2024); OECD Family Database SF2.3; Birenbaum-Carmeli (2016)
- Channel: tempo tau (earlier start leaves room for higher parity) + childlessness rho (near-universal reproduction)
- Classification: STRUCTURAL/outcome - the demographic footprint of the norm plus IVF
- Transferability: PARTIAL - earlier tempo and lower childlessness are outcomes of the norm and ART; ART is copyable, tempo is norm-gated

## INTERACTION NOTES (for the notebook build)

- The mechanisms are NOT independent - IVF (2), dual-earner (5), marriage (9) and low childlessness (10) are all downstream expressions of the SAME familism-nationalism norm (1, 4). Do not stack them additively; most of the "Israel bundle" is one super-channel (N) plus one compounding sub-population (Haredi/settlers)
- Composition vs lever: mechanisms 3 and 8 (Haredi, aliyah) are COMPOSITION; scoring them as transferable levers double-counts and inflates. Model Haredi/settlers as Leslie sub-populations with lambda>1 and leakage delta, per E33
- The copyable structural residual is small: universal IVF (~4% of births) + childcare/second-shift sharing. Everything else is norm (INHERENT-CULTURAL) or composition. This bounds the "import Israel" lever near the E20/E22 harbinger levels, not near a full 1-child recovery
- Attribution test for the notebook: hold N fixed and apply only the structural levers (IVF + childcare) to Korea/Japan/Italy - the residual should be a fraction of Israel's gap. If a naive fanout shows the Israel bundle recovering a collapsing country, it has laundered the INHERENT norm into a STRUCTURAL dial - the interaction-analyst's exact failure mode
