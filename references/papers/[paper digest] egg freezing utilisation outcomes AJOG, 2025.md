# [Paper digest] Elective Fertility Preservation: A National Database Study on Trends in Oocyte Cryopreservation and Oocyte Utilization Over a 5- to 7-Year Follow-up

**Original (link)**: [https://www.ajog.org/article/S0002-9378(25)00589-7/fulltext](https://www.ajog.org/article/S0002-9378(25)00589-7/fulltext)<br>

**Used in**: E18-H153 (egg freezing is a weak population-level fertility hedge)

## Summary

National-database study of elective (planned) oocyte cryopreservation, tracking how many women actually return to thaw and use their frozen eggs over a 5-7 year window - the real-world utilisation gap behind the "freeze now, use later" promise.

## Key findings

- Only ~5.7% of patients returned to use their cryopreserved oocytes within 5-7 years
- Return highest among older freezers - ~7.9% at ages 38-40 and ~8.0% at ages 41-42
- Among those who did thaw, live-birth outcomes were comparable to national IVF rates (procedure works when used)
- Combining low return with per-use success implies a live birth per freezer on the order of ~1.6% over the follow-up - most frozen eggs are never used within the window

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H153: elective egg freezing is not, at population scale, a meaningful fertility restorer. Utilisation is the binding constraint - the technology is efficacious per cycle but the overwhelming majority of freezers never return, so the realised birth yield is tiny. Egg freezing shifts option value and timing perception more than it lifts completed fertility.
