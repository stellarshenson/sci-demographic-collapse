# [Paper digest] Examining the Relationship Between Financial Issues and Divorce

**Authors**: Jeffrey Dew, Sonya Britt, Sandra Huston<br>
**Year**: 2012  **Venue**: Family Relations 61(4): 615-628<br>
**Original (source)**: [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1741-3729.2012.00715.x](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1741-3729.2012.00715.x) (DOI 10.1111/j.1741-3729.2012.00715.x)<br>

**Grounds**: E17-H132 (money disagreement as the leading modifiable divorce predictor)

## Key mechanism

It is financial disagreements - the arguing - not the level of financial well-being that drives divorce. Money fights outrank other disagreement domains and predict dissolution net of income and assets. The effect runs through mediators (conflict tactics from systems theory, marital satisfaction from social-exchange theory), i.e. money arguments corrode the relationship rather than poverty ending it directly.

## Main findings

- Longitudinal NSFH data, N = 4,574 couples, both wife- and husband-reported
- Financial disagreements were the strongest disagreement type (vs household, sex, in-laws, time) predicting later divorce for both spouses
- Once financial disagreements were in the model, financial well-being (assets, income) was no longer associated with divorce
- Conflict tactics and marital satisfaction fully mediated the disagreement -> divorce hazard

## Key takeaways

- The modifiable target is money conflict, not money itself - argument frequency, not the balance sheet, is the lever
- Explains why cash transfers alone need not stabilize unions: transfers raise well-being but do not resolve disagreement dynamics
- Pairs with the PREP / relationship-education digests: financial-communication skills are a plausible high-yield content for couple education
