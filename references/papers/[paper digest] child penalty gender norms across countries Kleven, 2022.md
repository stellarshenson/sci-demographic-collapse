## Summary

Event-study estimates of the child penalty - the drop in women's earnings/employment at first birth relative to men - across many countries, and the role of gender norms.

## Key findings

- The US long-run child penalty is ~31% in earnings; employment penalties 20-24%, persisting 5-10 years
- Penalties range widely by place and norm (21% in low-penalty regions to 61% in high-norm regions)
- The penalty concentrates in married women (27% vs ~5% for single) - it is norm-driven, not biological
- The penalty accounts for most of the residual gender pay gap in high-income countries

## Relevance to this project

The anchor for H110: because the penalty is norm-driven, an individual "lean-in" is dominated by the prevailing local norm, so only a universal (not opt-in) removal of the penalty pulls births earlier and cuts per-child cost.
