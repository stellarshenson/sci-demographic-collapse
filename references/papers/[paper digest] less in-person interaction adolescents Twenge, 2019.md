# [Paper digest] Less In-Person Social Interaction with Peers Among US Adolescents in the 21st Century and Links to Loneliness

**Authors**: Jean M. Twenge, Brian H. Spitzberg, W. Keith Campbell<br>
**Year**: 2019  **Venue**: Journal of Social and Personal Relationships (SAGE)<br>
**Original (download)**: [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0265407519836170](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0265407519836170)<br>
**Local PDF**: not downloaded - original is paywalled or render-blocked; use the link above<br>
**Used in**: E15-H64 (social-media time regulation - time displacement)

## Summary

Trend analysis of US adolescents' in-person socializing as digital media use rose.

## Key findings

- In-person peer interaction fell sharply among US adolescents this century
- The decline tracks the rise of smartphones/social media and links to loneliness
- Digital socializing displaces, rather than adds to, face-to-face contact
