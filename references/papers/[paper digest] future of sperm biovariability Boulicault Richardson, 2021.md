## Summary

The critique paired with Levine's Sperm Count Decline (SCD) meta-regression. Proposes a Sperm Count Biovariability (SCB) framework: sperm count varies across a wide, largely non-pathological, species-typical range, so a declining population mean need not signal a fertility or health crisis.

## Key findings

- Identifies weaknesses in the SCD hypothesis: the pathological "healthy threshold" is an assumption, not established; trends rest on non-representative, Western-skewed convenience samples
- Regional heterogeneity in the underlying data undercuts a uniform worldwide decline
- Under SCB, much within- and between-population variation in count is normal and not linked to reduced fecundity
- Argues the count-to-fertility link is weak in the range where most men sit; a mean shift may carry little reproductive consequence

## Relevance to this project

Grounds the sceptical half of H154. Paired with the Levine 2022 digest (which reports the ~51.6% concentration / ~62.3% count decline), it justifies a PARTIAL/contested verdict: the biological-fertility channel from falling sperm counts is real in the data but its causal weight on realised fertility is disputed and probably small relative to behavioural and structural drivers.
