## Summary

Tests the Lutz "low-fertility-trap" hypothesis in China: growing up as an only child lowers the number of children a person later considers ideal, so a coercive one-generation policy imprints small-family preferences that persist after the policy ends.

## Key findings

- Fuzzy regression-discontinuity using the 1980 one-child-policy introduction as a natural experiment, on cohorts born around 1980
- Being an only child significantly reduces the ideal number of children and the probability of wanting two or more
- The sibship-size effect is a plausible micro-mechanism for entrenched low fertility ideals in urban China
- Consistent with the observed failure of the policy reversal: TFR did not rebound after the one-child policy was lifted (Chinese TFR fell further, ~1.67 to ~1.09), evidencing hysteresis rather than a reversible constraint

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H156: preferences are path-dependent. A cohort raised in small families becomes a generation that wants small families, so lifting the constraint does not restore fertility - the norm is now endogenous. This is the hysteresis case for why late interventions fail and why demographic momentum has a preference-side analogue.
