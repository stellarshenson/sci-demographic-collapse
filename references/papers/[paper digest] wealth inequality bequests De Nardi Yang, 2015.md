## Summary

A quantitative model of wealth accumulation, voluntary bequests and estate taxation, calibrated to US wealth-inequality data.

## Key findings

- Bequests behave as luxury goods - concentrated among the rich and rising sharply with wealth
- Bequest motives and inherited human capital drive the persistence of wealth inequality across generations
- Estate taxation reduces the wealth of the richest and the labour supply of heirs
- The dynastic transfer channel is a major amplifier of top-wealth concentration

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H106/H107: bequests fund the dynastic education spending that raises the stakes of the status race (Becker-Tomes quantity-quality); an estate tax targets the resource the arms race feeds on, but leaks through legal avoidance and dents heir labour and mobile capital (the side cost stacked in the frontier).
