## Summary

A model of parenting style (permissive, authoritative, authoritarian) as a rational response to economic incentives, matched to cross-country and historical variation.

## Key findings

- Parenting intensity tracks the skill/income premium - high inequality → intensive "authoritative" parenting
- Low-inequality countries (Sweden) are permissive; high-inequality (US, China) are intensive
- The stakes of a child's rank, not the price of a child, drive the intensive-parenting cost
- The mechanism is self-reinforcing through the return to education

## Relevance to this project

The theoretical backbone of Act I and H105: compressing inequality lowers the return to rank and deflates the education arms race for everyone at once, with no per-family enforcement - the strongest and most defection-robust lever.
