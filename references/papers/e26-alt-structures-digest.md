# [Paper digest] E26 - Alternative mating, kinship and cultural structures as fertility levers

**Batch**: E26 contrarian fanout, hypotheses H188-H207<br>
**Model channels fed**: {fS, fC, fPb, fTau, fRV, fN} on the coupled behavioural x Leslie model (C coupling, rho childlessness, Pbar parity/quantum, tau tempo, S security, N bistable norm state)<br>
**Framing**: alternative structures (polyamory / matriliny / communalism / normalisation / kin-density / state-support variants) as levers. Honest brief - most contrarian structural levers turn out dominated. The meta-finding is that the few that work are not novel structures at all: they are the campaign's existing cost-socialisation, kin-proximity and norm channels re-derived under new labels.

## Why this round is contrarian and what it tests

The demographic-collapse literature the campaign has already digested (Doepke-Zilibotti inequality, Kleven child penalty, Bauernschuster childcare, Kearney-Wilson marriageable men, kibbutz cost of children) points to cost, opportunity cost and norms as the binding constraints. This round asks whether re-engineering the FORM of the mating/kinship unit - non-dyadic partnering, female-line descent, collective childrearing, plural legal parenthood, engineered kin density, media-driven norm shifts - can move fertility independent of those constraints. The recurring answer: the structure moves fertility only through one of the already-known channels (marginal cost of a child, deliverable childcare, the norm state N), and the exotic wrapper usually adds fragility, leakage or an autonomy cost that dominates it.

## Cluster 1 - Polyamory / consensual non-monogamy (CNM)

- Prevalence is real but the engaged share is small and demographically skewed. Kinsey Institute / Moors et al.: ~1 in 5 US singles have ever tried CNM, 16.8% desire polyamory, 10.7% have ever engaged; concentrated among men, sexual minorities, urban and educated adults - i.e. exactly the lower-fertility strata (Kinsey Institute 2023; Moors et al. 2021)
- No study shows CNM raises completed fertility. The POLYBABES work (Arseneau et al. 2019/2021) is qualitative on healthcare experience; ~23% of Canadian CNM adults have a child at home - not elevated. The 2024 Wiley scoping review (Gupta et al.) confirms family-formation data on CNM is thin and finds no fertility premium
- Mechanistically ambiguous on C: concurrency can raise the count of partnerships but dilutes the dyadic commitment that predicts childbearing (Toulemon 2012 union-stability logic). Male reproductive variance rises (high-status men monopolise mates), the classic polygyny-instability externality (Henrich et al. 2012 on monogamy's normative advantage)
- Verdict lean: REFUTED as a fertility lever; PARTIAL only for the alloparenting sub-mechanism (see H189), which is really cost-sharing, not non-monogamy

## Cluster 2 - Matriarchy / matrilineal descent (Mosuo, Minangkabau, Khasi, Akan)

- Matriliny is daughter-biased investment: descent, inheritance and childrearing support run through the female line (Holden, Sear & Mace 2003, "Matriliny as daughter-biased investment", EHB). It raises women's autonomy and lowers childlessness (near-universal reproduction), and maternal kin let daughters breed earlier (tempo down)
- But it does NOT raise quantum. Mattison et al. on the Mosuo: wealth raises female reproductive success, yet co-resident female kin COMPETE - each additional co-resident female relative lowers a woman's reproductive success (Mattison 2011 Human Nature "matrilineal puzzle"; Mattison et al. 2016 sex-biased investment). Ji et al. (2013): communal breeding by women is associated with LOWER husband investment. Mosuo TFR is not high
- Contrarian core: the "stack maternal kin to pool childcare" intuition is antagonistic beyond a small optimum - same-sex kin crowding turns cooperative into competitive
- The transferable, autonomy-positive signal is narrow: matrilocal residence (living near the wife's mother) delivers deliverable grandmaternal childcare, which is a real cost-of-children lever - but that is the grandmother channel (Cluster 5), not descent rules
- Verdict lean: PARTIAL for matrilocal kin proximity; REFUTED for "matriliny raises fertility"; non-transferable as a whole kinship system

## Cluster 3 - Communal / collective childrearing (kibbutz, cooperative breeding)

- Cleanest natural experiment in the campaign. Kibbutzim socialised the full marginal cost of a child (food, daycare, housing scaled to family size) and sustained ABOVE Israeli-Jewish-average fertility until the late-1980s crisis; the usual negative education-fertility gradient flattened inside the kibbutz (Anson & Meir 1996, J Population Economics, "Equality and fertility in the kibbutz")
- Privatisation 1996-2005 shifted child costs back onto parents: lifetime fertility fell by 0.65 children (6-15%) even though the communal FORM persisted (Ebenstein, Hazan & Simhon 2016, Economic Journal). This is the key identification - the operative channel is cost-socialisation (fPb), not communalism per se
- Hrdy's cooperative-breeding hypothesis (Hrdy 2009, Mothers and Others; Kramer) argues human high fertility-plus-survival depends on allomothers. Sear & Mace 2008 ("Who keeps children alive?", EHB, 45 populations): maternal grandmothers and elder-sibling helpers raise child survival in most studies; fathers help in only ~1/3. But the fertility-quantum boost is contested - Strassmann & Kurapati 2010 (BBS) find most natural-fertility populations do NOT support a grandmother/cooperative-breeding fertility effect
- State-support variant works and is scalable: universal subsidised under-3 childcare raised German fertility ~2.8% (Bauernschuster, Hener & Rainer 2016, JEEA); Rindfuss et al. Norway positive. Modest, causal, in-kind - consistent with the campaign's in-kind-beats-cash prior
- Verdict lean: SUPPORTED for cost-socialisation and for state-funded childcare; PARTIAL for generic cooperative breeding (survival robust, fertility contested; works through committed kin, not generic community)

## Cluster 4 - Cultural shaping / normalisation (the norm state N)

- The norm channel N is causal and well identified - but the identified evidence runs ANTINATAL. La Ferrara, Chong & Duryea 2012 (AEJ:Applied, "Soap Operas and Fertility"): Globo telenovela coverage lowered Brazilian fertility, strongest for low-SES women and stopping behaviour, with novela-content and child-naming evidence; 72% of lead female characters were childless. Kearney & Levine 2015 (AER): MTV's 16 and Pregnant cut teen births 5.7%, one-third of the post-2009 decline
- These two are the best empirical anchors for the model's bistable-N forcing term and the rho_target += 0.30*(N-N0) coupling - use them for CALIBRATION of the norm channel, not as levers
- Symmetric PRONATAL media efficacy is unproven and likely weaker (reactance; the campaign's own propaganda-backfire finding). Israel's pronatalism is not a media campaign - it is a whole-society norm
- Israel as existence proof: Jewish TFR 2.65 to 3.06 (2002-2022); even SECULAR Jewish women TFR 1.96 vs ~1.0 for US secular Jews; pronatal norms cut across religiosity and education (Taub Center "Israel's Exceptional Fertility"; Hess 2024). Endogenous national-identity endowment (nation-building, religious continuity), not an exportable dial - but it bounds the MAXIMUM the N channel can deliver in a rich country
- The inter-channel finding: N is a barrier-lowering multiplier. A pronatal N makes structural levers (childcare, kin) cheaper and stickier to adopt, which is why Israel's average-strength policy "punches above weight" while Poland's cash+childcare failed without a norm shift. Fertility-contagion among peers grounds the slope mechanism (Balbo & Barban 2014)
- Verdict lean: SUPPORTED for N as a causal calibration channel and as a derivative multiplier; PARTIAL for deliberate pronatal media (direction asymmetry, reactance)

## Cluster 5 - Grandmother / kin-density / alloparent

- Maternal grandmother proximity is a quantified fertility input. Engelhardt et al. 2019 (Current Biology): a living maternal grandmother raised her daughter's children born by ~2.1, benefit decaying with distance over 0-325 km - proximity, not mere survival, drives it. Lahdenperä et al. 2004 (Nature): post-reproductive grandmothers give daughters more grandchildren by letting them breed earlier and more frequently. Sear & Mace 2008 confirms the maternal-grandmother survival effect across populations
- Contested arm: Strassmann & Kurapati 2010 (Dogon, BBS) - many natural-fertility studies show weak or absent grandmother fertility effects; the effect is helper-quality and context dependent
- The channel erodes exactly when it is needed: modern residential dispersion plus grandmother labour-force participation collapse deliverable grandmaternal care at the same time tempo is latest. Kin buffers the fecundability loss at late tau (a tempo x kin derivative interaction), but that buffer is disappearing
- "Buying out" the grandmother (paying grandparents to provide care - Korea/Hungary proposals) does not reliably recreate proximity or the commitment that gives kin care its value; monetising kin is a weak substitute
- Verdict lean: SUPPORTED for kin proximity as a real input (but an endowment, not a policy dial, and shrinking); PARTIAL/REFUTED for contested generic grandmother effects and for grandmother-buyout schemes

## Cluster 6 - State-support synthesis and the autonomy frontier

- The state-pays test flips several verdicts: legal recognition of plural parents (California SB274 2013; British Columbia Family Law Act 2013) is a genuine institutional innovation but is scoped to child security for LGBT and blended families, touches <1% of births, and shows no quantum effect - REFUTED as a fertility lever. State-funded childcare (Cluster 3) is the state-support variant that DOES work
- Complementarity: cost-socialisation (fPb), kin proximity (fPb via a different source) and a pronatal norm (fN) act on different channels and stack super-additively - the kibbutz worked because it sat inside a pronatal-national N; childcare plus a shifted norm beats either alone
- Substitutes that saturate: state childcare and grandmother care buy down the SAME mother's-opportunity-cost, so they partly crowd out each other (Bauernschuster notes informal-care crowd-out)
- The dominating side-effect axis is women's autonomy. Structures that raise quantum by LOWERING autonomy (coercive-pronatal, forced-universal-early-marriage, patriarchal traditionalism) erode the gender-equity-in-the-home lever the campaign already identified as a root cause (Doepke-Kindermann; Kleven gender-norm child penalty). The only autonomy-positive structural winners are childcare-socialisation, kin proximity and gender-equity - which are not exotic structures at all
