## Summary

Evaluation of Hungary's large family-policy package (tax exemptions, CSOK housing loan) and its fertility impact.

## Key findings

- Hungary spends up to 6.2% of GDP on family support; TFR rose 1.23 (2011) to ~1.61 (2021)
- The upturn is 'largely a slowdown in the shift to later parenthood' - a tempo effect
- Cohort and tempo-adjusted fertility barely moved - not a durable quantum gain

## Relevance to this project

Grounds the PARTIAL verdict on H74: even a 6.2%-of-GDP tax-and-loan push buys mostly timing, not completed families.
