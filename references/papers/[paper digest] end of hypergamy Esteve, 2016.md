## Summary

Documents the global reversal of the educational gender gap - young women now out-educate young men across most of the world - and its consequence for female educational hypergamy (women marrying up), the historical marriage norm.

## Key findings

- By 2010 the share of women aged 25-29 with college exceeded that of men in 139+ countries, covering ~86% of world population
- Hypergamy (women partnering up in education) is no longer demographically feasible at scale; the pool of "higher-educated" men has shrunk relative to educated women
- Projections imply a shift toward homogamy and rising female hypogamy (women partnering down)
- Where the norm that men should out-earn/out-educate persists, the mismatch produces a marriage squeeze for highly educated women and less-educated men

## Relevance to this project

Grounds H155: the reversed education gradient breaks the traditional matching market. If hypergamy norms are sticky, a growing bloc of educated women and low-education men fail to match, depressing marriage and (in marriage-centred fertility regimes like East Asia) births. A structural marriage-market undercurrent distinct from cost or policy levers.
