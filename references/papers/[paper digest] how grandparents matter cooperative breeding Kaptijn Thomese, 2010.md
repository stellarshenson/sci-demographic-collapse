# [Paper digest] How Grandparents Matter: support for the cooperative breeding hypothesis in a contemporary Dutch population

**Authors**: Ralf Kaptijn, Fleur Thomese, Theo G. van Tilburg, Aart C. Liefbroer<br>
**Year**: 2010  **Venue**: Human Nature 21(4), 393-405<br>
**Original (full text, open)**: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2995872/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2995872/)<br>
**Local PDF**: not downloaded - Springer PDF is gated; PMC full-text HTML above is open<br>
**Used in**: E46 - kin-childcare -> next-birth effect size in a contemporary developed setting

## Summary

Tests the cooperative-breeding hypothesis in a modern low-fertility country: does grandparental childcare raise the probability that adult children have (more) children? Uses three-generation longitudinal data from the Living Arrangements and Social Networks of Older Adults survey (Netherlands), grandparents aged 55+ in 1992, followed 8-10 years to 2000/2002. Sample of 572 adult children (265 already parents, 307 childless).

## Key findings

- **For parents already having children, frequent grandparental childcare raised the predicted probability of a further birth from 35% to 66% (a ~31 percentage-point increase) over the 8-10 year follow-up**; logit coefficient 1.286 (p < 0.05) for the "often" category
- Occasional childcare showed a positive but non-significant trend
- **For childless adults, grandparental childcare given to a sibling's children had no significant effect** on their own transition to parenthood - the effect is specific to *subsequent* births by direct recipients of help
- Interpreted as support for cooperative breeding: kin help enhances descendants' reproductive success even in a modern welfare state
- Caveat: attrition through grandparent mortality (73% of losses) may inflate the estimate

## Relevance to this project

Together with Zhang-Emery (China, ~4x odds), this is the developed-country anchor for the kin-childcare mechanism: available grandparental care sharply raises progression to the *next* birth but does not manufacture *first* births in the childless. The mechanism is a parity-progression / cost-reduction effect, not a coupling or partnership effect - which is why it should enter the model on the security/cost side rather than as a driver of entry into parenthood.
