## Summary

The economics of parenting styles: why intensive parenting rises with inequality.

## Key findings

- Intensive, achievement-oriented ('tiger'/'helicopter') parenting is a rational response to high inequality
- When the gap between success and failure is large, parents invest unlimited time and money per child
- This raises the effective cost of a child and lowers fertility; low-inequality societies parent more relaxedly

## Relevance to this project

The theoretical root of the deeper-drivers chapter and the top-rated lever H90: to lower the child-cost arms race, compress the winner-take-all stakes rather than subsidize the race.
