# [Paper digest] Male Earnings, Marriageable Men, and Nonmarital Fertility: Evidence from the Fracking Boom

**Authors**: Melissa S. Kearney, Riley Wilson<br>

**Original (download)**: [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23408/w23408.pdf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23408/w23408.pdf) (open NBER working paper)<br>

## Key mechanism

Tests the reverse of the Wilson hypothesis: if declining male earnings drove the retreat from marriage and the rise in non-marital births, then a positive shock to less-educated men's earnings should raise marriage and cut non-marital births. Uses localised fracking booms as an exogenous, male-skewed income shock to non-college men, then reads off births and marriage. The result splits the "marriageable men" idea: money raises births but does not, by itself, rebuild marriage - social context governs whether the extra births are marital.

## Main findings

- \$1,000 of fracking production per capita raises average earnings ~3.8% for non-college men, and the wage gain is persistent (about two-thirds still present two years out)
- The same shock raises total births by ~5.96 per 1,000 women; both marital and non-marital birth rates rise ~3-3.6 per 1,000 (~3%) - a positive income effect on childbearing
- No detectable increase in marriage rates - the extra earnings do not translate into more marriage
- Contrast with the 1970s-80s Appalachian coal boom, where a comparable male-earnings shock raised marriage rates and marital births and lowered non-marital births - same economic force, opposite family-structure outcome, because social norms shifted between eras
- Effects concentrated among non-Hispanic whites; larger non-marital response where non-marital births were already a bigger share

## Key takeaways

- Male income has a real, positive causal effect on fertility - money → more babies is confirmed
- But money alone does not restore marriage in the modern normative context; the marriage response is context-dependent, not mechanical
- The coal-vs-fracking contrast is the central caution: a pro-natal earnings lever will not automatically deliver marital, two-parent births - the social frame determines the family form
