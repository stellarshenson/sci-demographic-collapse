5. **Task [Extended] - Notebook 4: tempo-quantum + Bayesian free-energy calibration (Round 2-3, E8-E9)** (v0.1.0): Separated the fertility signal into tempo vs quantum and calibrated it by variational free-energy minimization<br>
   **Result**: E8 applies Bongaarts-Feeney `adjTFR = TFR/(1−r)`, `r = d(MAC)/dt` from the UN WPP mean-age-at-childbearing series, splitting each region's shortfall into recoverable postponement vs structural collapse: the USA quantum is ~2.18 (its low period TFR is largely *tempo* - births postponed, near replacement once timing is removed) while Korea's quantum is ~1.55 (a *structural* deficit); a skew-normal located at the mean age reconstructs the single-year ASFR to MAE 2.9/1000 (2.8% of peak), giving E9 a two-parameter driver. For E9 the validated Leslie core was factored into `src/sci_demographic_collapse/coremodel.py` (imported by NB4+), and `pyro-ppl`+`arviz` were added via `make install`. The quantum trajectory is fit with a local-linear-trend state-space model by Pyro SVI, which minimizes the variational free energy `F=−ELBO`. Two debugging turns mattered: first the credible band was computed over the mean function (32% coverage) and had to become the posterior *predictive* including observation noise σ (→100% in-sample); then a smooth exponential trend gave 0% backtest coverage because its extrapolation band does not widen, so it was replaced by a stochastic trend whose forecast uncertainty grows with horizon. The headline, honest result: held-out (train ≤2015) *population* MAPE is ≤3% everywhere (USA 1.16%, Korea 2.58%, Italy 0.26%), but period-TFR point-forecast coverage is regime-dependent (Korea 88% monotone-forecastable, USA 12% because the post-2015 fertility drop is a genuine regime change no prior data anticipates) - momentum and age structure make population predictable while period TFR is not. Posterior parameter table (means ± CI, `F` at optimum) and a prediction-vs-observed residual table were exported to `reports/`. Outcome **5 SUPPORTED, 1 PARTIAL**. 6. **Task [Extended] - Notebook 5: crisis battery + counterfactual costs (Round 4, E10)** (v0.1.0): Stress-tested the age-structured model against four past crises and quantified each one's demographic cost by counterfactual<br>
   **Result**: The SOTA bar demands past events survive scrutiny, so NB5 runs the 2008 Great Recession, COVID-19, Korea's 1997 IMF crisis and German reunification through the calibrated core. Two counterfactual machines were added: a fertility counterfactual (hold the Bongaarts-Feeney quantum flat at the pre-crisis level, rescale ASFR, re-run the Leslie projection → births forgone) and a mortality counterfactual (needed a small surgical `sx_override` added to `coremodel.project` - hold pre-crisis survival over the crisis years → excess deaths). A first linear-extrapolation counterfactual gave negative forgone births (extrapolating a 7-year quantum trend over 11+ years is unstable), so it was replaced by the robust hold-flat construction. Results are sharp and honest: COVID reproduces as a **mortality** shock - model excess deaths 985k over 2020-21, matching the real ≈1M - with a fertility dip that was mostly recoverable tempo (rebounded by 2022). The 2008 recession's immediate 2007-2013 TFR fall (−0.26) was almost entirely **tempo** (postponement, only 4% quantum), which contradicted the pre-registered "predominantly quantum" prediction (→ PARTIAL, an honest miss) but never recovered, so it still cost ~2.8M births cumulatively. Korea 1997 is a **permanent quantum step** (92% quantum, no recovery through 2005, ~3.15M forgone) and reunification shows the East German collapse in the national aggregate (~0.72M forgone; sub-national split flagged). The synthesis ranks crises by kind: mortality (COVID) vs fertility (Korea > recession > reunification). The tempo-quantum lens is what makes the distinction crisp - recoverable postponement vs permanent loss - the same axis that decides whether an intervention can work. Outcome **4 SUPPORTED, 1 PARTIAL**. Artefacts: `notebooks/05-kj-demographic-crises.ipynb` (exit 0), `coremodel.project` `sx_override`, four figures, `reports/nb5_crisis_costs.csv`, verdicts JSON, and E10 filled in the experiments log.
